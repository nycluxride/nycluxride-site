Chauffeur service at JFK Airport

Flight-timed pickups at every JFK passenger terminal, one flat rate to or from any New York City address, and 45 minutes of complimentary waiting on domestic arrivals, 60 on international.

Every terminal on one loop road

JFK lies in Jamaica, on the northern shore of Jamaica Bay in southeastern Queens, about sixteen miles southeast of Midtown Manhattan. In September 2026 passengers use Terminals 1, 4, 5, 7 and 8, arranged around a single loop road, and that loop is fed from the north by the Van Wyck Expressway and from the west by the JFK Expressway off the Belt Parkway. A new Terminal One and a new Terminal 6 are under construction and will change that list as they open. The AirTrain runs the same circuit and links the terminals to Jamaica Station and Howard Beach, which matters if your party is splitting up on arrival.

Each terminal has its own arrivals level and its own set of doors, and the buildings are far enough apart that a car sent to the wrong one is not a short walk away. The terminal number is therefore the single most useful thing to give when booking, followed by the airline and the flight number. If you do not know the terminal, the flight number is enough to find it.

How the pickup is arranged

JFK keeps its terminal curbs for active pickups only, so a car cannot stand and wait at the arrivals door. The standard pickup is at the curb or the designated pickup area for your terminal. At Terminals 5 and 7, car service pickups use a lot at the Howard Beach AirTrain station, a free AirTrain ride from the terminal. The chauffeur holds nearby and confirms the exact spot with you by phone or WhatsApp once you have your bags and are on the arrivals level. Nothing about that is left to chance on the day, and you are not asked to hunt for a car in a moving line of traffic. The guide to meeting your driver at the airport covers the messages that make it quick.

If you would rather not coordinate at all, add meet and greet. The chauffeur waits inside the terminal near baggage claim with a name sign and takes the bags from there. It is an add-on plus any airport parking charge. Arrivals are tracked in real time and the pickup time moves with the aircraft, so a delayed flight does not cost you the car. Domestic arrivals carry 45 minutes of complimentary waiting and international arrivals 60 minutes, counted from touchdown. Every other pickup carries a 15 minute grace period.

Where the trips go from the terminals

Manhattan is the common destination, and the route depends on which end of the island you are headed for. Midtown is the Van Wyck Expressway north to the Long Island Expressway and then the Queens-Midtown Tunnel. The Financial District and Battery Park City are usually quicker the other way, west along the Belt Parkway to the Hugh L. Carey Tunnel. For the Upper East Side, the Van Wyck to the Grand Central Parkway and then the Robert F. Kennedy Bridge onto the FDR Drive skips Midtown traffic completely.

Brooklyn is west on the Belt Parkway, or out through Conduit Boulevard onto Atlantic Avenue for the northern half of the borough. Long Island City and western Queens come off the Van Wyck and the Long Island Expressway. The Bronx is north on the Whitestone Expressway and over the Bronx-Whitestone Bridge. Staten Island is the Belt Parkway to the Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge.

East and north of the field

Not every arrival here is going into Manhattan. Rockaway Boulevard and Cross Bay Boulevard serve Howard Beach and the Rockaway peninsula on the airport's own doorstep, which are short trips that still need a booking because no other car is going to be waiting for them. Eastbound, the field opens onto the Nassau Expressway and the Southern State Parkway, the roads that lead out of the city toward Long Island.

Northbound the run goes up the Van Wyck to the Whitestone Expressway and over the Bronx-Whitestone Bridge into the Bronx. Worth knowing when a departure is being timed: the Kew Gardens interchange, where the Van Wyck meets the Grand Central Parkway and the Jackie Robinson Parkway, is where most northbound delay actually forms, several miles short of the terminals rather than at them.

Luggage and larger parties

When the bags outnumber the seats

A long-haul arrival at Terminal 1 or Terminal 4 often means more cases than a sedan can take. The Business SUV class carries five passengers with three large and three carry-on bags. The twelve seat Sprinter carries ten large bags with two carry-ons, which is usually the answer for a family arriving together or a group heading to one hotel. Say how many cases you have when you book and the right class is assigned rather than guessed at on the curb.

Asked at the terminals

What to settle before you land

Where exactly will the chauffeur be waiting?

Close by, because JFK keeps its terminal curbs for active pickups only. You are met at the curb or the designated pickup area for your terminal, and the precise spot is confirmed by phone or WhatsApp once you are on the arrivals level with your bags. At Terminals 5 and 7 that area is a lot at the Howard Beach AirTrain station, a free AirTrain ride from the terminal. With meet and greet, an add-on plus any airport parking charge, the answer is simpler, because the chauffeur is already inside near baggage claim with a name sign when you come through.

Which terminals do you cover?

Every passenger terminal in use. In September 2026 those are 1, 4, 5, 7 and 8, and the list changes as the new Terminal One and Terminal 6 open. Departures are dropped at the terminal you name. Arrivals are met at the curb or the designated pickup area for your terminal, or inside near baggage claim if you have added meet and greet. Give the flight number when you book and both ends are set before the day.

What happens if my flight is late?

The flight is watched in real time and the pickup is rescheduled to the new arrival time at no extra cost. Once the aircraft is on the ground, domestic arrivals have 45 minutes of complimentary waiting and international arrivals have 60, which is enough for immigration, baggage and customs on most days. If you clear early, call and the car comes forward.

Where does traffic build on the Van Wyck?

Mostly at the Kew Gardens interchange, where the Van Wyck Expressway meets the Grand Central Parkway and the Jackie Robinson Parkway, several miles short of the terminals rather than at them. The fare stays flat whatever the traffic does.
