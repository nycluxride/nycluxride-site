The largest borough, with two airports in it

Queens is the largest borough, and both city airports are here. LaGuardia is in East Elmhurst on the north shore and JFK is in Jamaica on the south side, about 10 miles apart. Which one is closer depends on where you start. From Astoria, Long Island City, Sunnyside or Jackson Heights, LaGuardia is much closer. From Jamaica, Ozone Park, Howard Beach or the Rockaways, JFK is. From Forest Hills, Flushing and Bayside the two airports are about the same distance away.

Water borders most of Queens. The East River and Long Island Sound are to the north, and Jamaica Bay and the Atlantic to the south. Nassau County is to the east and Brooklyn to the southwest. Queens has dozens of neighborhoods. Tell us the neighborhood as well as the street.

How Queens addresses work

Most Queens roads are numbered, and the same number is used several times. One number can be an avenue, a road, a drive, a place and a street, all within a few blocks. The first part of a house number, like the 34 in 34-12, points to the nearest cross street. It helps, but please give us the cross street too.

The big roads have names. Queens Boulevard goes through the middle from the Queensboro Bridge to Jamaica, and Northern Boulevard follows the north side. Astoria Boulevard is next to the road into LaGuardia. Woodhaven Boulevard crosses the borough north to south, Hillside Avenue and Union Turnpike serve the east, and Cross Bay Boulevard goes over Jamaica Bay to the Rockaways.

Expressways and parkways

The Grand Central Parkway goes to LaGuardia and the Van Wyck Expressway goes to JFK. The Long Island Expressway leads to Manhattan through the Queens-Midtown Tunnel, and the Brooklyn-Queens Expressway (BQE) leads to Brooklyn. On the edges, the Cross Island Parkway follows the eastern shore and the Clearview Expressway comes down from the Throgs Neck Bridge. The Whitestone Expressway links the Bronx-Whitestone Bridge to the Van Wyck, and the Belt Parkway follows Jamaica Bay past JFK toward Brooklyn. The Jackie Robinson Parkway cuts across the middle from Brooklyn to Kew Gardens.

Newark Liberty and Teterboro are harder from Queens. You have to cross Manhattan, or go the long way through Brooklyn and Staten Island. For a flight from New Jersey, leave plenty of extra time, and book an early pickup if you can.

Stadiums and colleges in Queens

Citi Field and the USTA Billie Jean King National Tennis Center are next to each other in Flushing Meadows Corona Park, with the Unisphere, the Queens Museum and the New York Hall of Science. From late August into September, US Open traffic jams every road in from the Grand Central Parkway and Roosevelt Avenue. Meet your car a few blocks from the main gates. Forest Hills Stadium is on narrow residential streets. Concert nights there work the same way. Aqueduct Racetrack and Resorts World are in South Ozone Park, by the Belt Parkway.

Astoria has Kaufman Astoria Studios and the Museum of the Moving Image. Jamaica has St. John's University and Jamaica Station, where the Long Island Rail Road, the subway and the JFK AirTrain meet. Queens College is in Flushing. Main Street and Roosevelt Avenue in Flushing are very busy and no car can wait there. Meet your car one street over.

Eastern Queens and the Rockaways

East of Flushing Meadows, Queens is suburban. Fresh Meadows, Queens Village, Hollis, St. Albans, Laurelton, Rosedale and Little Neck have quiet streets, served by Utopia Parkway, Francis Lewis Boulevard, Kissena Boulevard and Springfield Boulevard. Cunningham Park and Alley Pond Park are between them, and Fort Totten is at the point under the Throgs Neck Bridge. At the far western end, Astoria Park is under the Hell Gate Bridge.

From the rest of Queens, you reach the Rockaways by Cross Bay Boulevard across Jamaica Bay, and from Brooklyn by the Marine Parkway Bridge. On the peninsula, Beach Channel Drive and the Rockaway Freeway go from Far Rockaway past Rockaway Beach and Jacob Riis Park to Breezy Point. On summer weekends, allow extra time. Each way in is a single bridge, with no other road.

Airport routes from Queens

Here are the usual routes from Queens to each airport. For arrivals, see LaGuardia terminal pickups and JFK terminal pickups. Our guide to airport routes from Brooklyn and Queens compares the BQE, the Belt Parkway and the Van Wyck.

Questions

Questions about Queens pickups

Which airport should I use from a Queens address?

It depends on your neighborhood. West and north of Flushing Meadows, LaGuardia is closer, and it costs less than JFK. South and east of the park, JFK is closer. Forest Hills, Flushing and Bayside are in between. There it depends on whether the Grand Central Parkway or the Van Wyck is moving that day.

Why does a Queens address need a cross street?

Because one number can be an avenue, a road, a drive, a place and a street, all within a few blocks. The first part of the house number points to the nearest cross street, but please give us the cross street anyway. Tell us which side of the street your entrance is on too, because many main roads here have no stopping on one side.

Can you pick me up at Citi Field or the US Open?

Yes. We agree on a meeting point when you book. When a game or session ends, the roads right around the stadium and the tennis center jam. A corner a few blocks out on Roosevelt Avenue, or on the far side of the park, gets you moving sooner than the nearest gate. For a whole evening with a stop before or after, book by the hour.

Which parts of Queens do you cover?

All of it. In the west: Astoria, Long Island City, Sunnyside and Woodside. Through the middle: Jackson Heights, Elmhurst, Corona and Rego Park. In the north and east: Flushing, College Point, Whitestone, Bayside and Douglaston. In the center: Forest Hills, Kew Gardens, Ridgewood, Maspeth and Middle Village. In the south: Jamaica, Ozone Park, Howard Beach, Broad Channel and the Rockaways. Long Island City has its own page.
