Chauffeur service in Long Island City

Flat rates to LaGuardia, JFK, Newark Liberty and Teterboro, from Court Square and Queens Plaza out to the Hunters Point waterfront.

One bridge and one tunnel from Midtown East

Long Island City holds the western edge of Queens, directly across the East River from Midtown East, and it is the closest part of the borough to Manhattan. Four crossings serve it. The Ed Koch Queensboro Bridge lands at Queens Plaza. The Queens-Midtown Tunnel surfaces near 21st Street and Borden Avenue. The Pulaski Bridge crosses Newtown Creek into Greenpoint, and the Kosciuszko Bridge carries the Brooklyn-Queens Expressway over the same creek a little further east. Astoria lies north across Broadway, Sunnyside sits behind the rail yard to the east, and the river closes the western side.

The Queensboro Bridge carries no toll and the Queens-Midtown Tunnel does, so the choice between them on a Manhattan trip is partly traffic and partly what you would rather pay. The bridge is also the slower of the two into Midtown at the end of the working day, when the Queens Plaza ramps fill from both directions at once.

Four sub-areas that behave differently

Hunters Point is the waterfront strip along Center Boulevard and 2nd Street, with the towers, the ferry landing at Hunters Point South and the two parks. Gantry Plaza State Park keeps the restored rail gantries and the Pepsi-Cola sign on the shoreline, and Hunters Point South Park runs south of it. Court Square is the office core around One Court Square, the tallest building in Queens. Queens Plaza carries the hotels and the bridge ramps. Dutch Kills sits between Queens Plaza and Sunnyside Yard, and Ravenswood runs north up Vernon Boulevard toward Astoria, the Noguchi Museum and Socrates Sculpture Park.

One local habit is worth knowing before you book. Queens numbers most of its roads instead of naming them, and it reuses each number with several different suffixes, so 44th Drive, 44th Road and 44th Avenue are three separate blocks within a few hundred feet of one another. Give the cross street alongside the number and the car arrives at the right door the first time. Vernon Boulevard, Jackson Avenue, Thomson Avenue and 21st Street are the through routes, and the bridge and tunnel ramps take up a great deal of the curb around Queens Plaza and Borden Avenue.

LaGuardia is about five miles up the road

LaGuardia is the near airport from here and one of the shortest airport runs available anywhere in the city. The usual route is the Brooklyn-Queens Expressway north to the Grand Central Parkway, with 21st Street and Astoria Boulevard as the surface alternative when the expressway is slow. JFK is the Long Island Expressway east to the Van Wyck Expressway and then south, roughly thirteen road miles.

Newark Liberty and Teterboro both run the other way, through the Queens-Midtown Tunnel and across Manhattan to the Lincoln Tunnel or the Holland Tunnel, which means two river crossings and the width of the island in between. A Newark departure from Long Island City deserves more margin than the distance suggests, and booking it early in the morning avoids the worst of both tunnels.

Offices, studios and hotel work

Weekday demand here is corporate. The office blocks along Jackson Avenue and around Court Square generate meeting and airport work, and the hotel cluster on Queens Plaza and Jackson Avenue fills with people who are in the city for a day or two and want a car rather than a search for one. Silvercup Studios brings production traffic with call times before dawn, and MoMA PS1 on Jackson Avenue brings evening arrivals. The waterfront event spaces on Center Boulevard and the restaurants on Vernon Boulevard carry the weekend load.

Long Island City station on the Long Island Rail Road and the Court Square subway interchange put a lot of people within a few blocks of each other at the same times of day, which is exactly when a named cross street earns its keep.

Ferries, rail and the water's edge

Long Island City has more ways off it than most districts of its size. The NYC Ferry landing at Hunters Point South connects to East 34th Street and to Wall Street, which on a bad afternoon can beat any road. Long Island City station and Hunters Point Avenue station serve the Long Island Rail Road, and the Court Square interchange gathers several subway lines under one block. The Roosevelt Island Bridge crosses from 36th Avenue in Astoria just north of the district.

The waterfront itself is still a working shoreline. Anable Basin cuts inland beside 44th Drive, Newtown Creek and the Dutch Kills basin close the southern edge, and the Greenpoint Avenue Bridge crosses the creek alongside the Pulaski. Center Boulevard, 5th Street and 11th Street run behind the towers, while Skillman Avenue and Queens Plaza North and South carry the traffic inland. Between the bridge ramps, the rail yard and the creek there are only a handful of ways through, so the cross street decides the approach rather than the destination.

Asked in LIC

What a booking needs on this side of the river

How close is LaGuardia from Long Island City?

About five road miles by the Brooklyn-Queens Expressway and the Grand Central Parkway, which makes it one of the shortest airport transfers in the city. The fare is one flat rate, set before the trip. No elapsed time is promised, because the Grand Central Parkway decides that, but the distance means a late departure is recoverable here in a way it is not from Brooklyn or the Bronx.

Why do you ask for a cross street here?

Because the numbering reuses itself. 44th Avenue, 44th Road and 44th Drive all exist within a short walk of one another, and the same is true of several other numbers in this part of Queens. A cross street resolves it instantly. It also helps to say whether your entrance faces the water, the plaza or a side street, since several of the newer towers on Center Boulevard have more than one.

Can a company hold an account for regular bookings?

Yes. Corporate accounts are billed monthly rather than per ride, which suits offices booking the same movements every week. Hourly work is billed by vehicle class with a three hour minimum, and airport transfers stay on the flat rates. All prices exclude taxes, tolls & gratuity, and the final total is confirmed at booking.

What counts as Long Island City for a booking?

Hunters Point, Court Square, Queens Plaza, Dutch Kills, Ravenswood and Blissville, which together fill the ground between the East River, Newtown Creek, Sunnyside Yard and Broadway. Astoria, Sunnyside and Woodside immediately outside that boundary fall under Queens coverage, and Greenpoint across the Pulaski Bridge under Brooklyn.
