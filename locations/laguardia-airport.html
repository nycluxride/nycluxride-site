Chauffeur service at LaGuardia Airport

One flat rate to or from any New York City address, with live flight tracking, a curbside pickup confirmed by phone or WhatsApp, and meet and greet inside the terminal as an add-on.

Two large terminals on a narrow site in East Elmhurst

LaGuardia occupies a tight strip of East Elmhurst between the Grand Central Parkway and the water of Flushing Bay and Bowery Bay. It is about eight or nine road miles from Midtown Manhattan, which makes it the nearest commercial airport to the middle of the city. Scheduled flights use Terminals B and C, the two large halls rebuilt in recent years, each with its own arrivals level and its own ramps off the parkway. Terminal A, the 1940 Marine Air Terminal at the western end of the field, is reached separately by Marine Terminal Road and now serves private aviation through Modern Aviation.

There is no rail line into the terminals, so every arrival and every departure here is a road movement. That one fact shapes the whole operation. All of the traffic in and out has to use the same handful of ramps, and at peak the queue begins before the terminal frontage rather than at it. A booking made with ten minutes of margin at LaGuardia is a booking made with none.

What to say when you book, and where you are met

The flight number is enough to find the terminal, and the airline and the terminal letter confirm it. The standard pickup is at the arrivals curb or the designated pickup area of that terminal. LaGuardia lets a car stop at the curb only once the passenger and the bags are there, so the chauffeur holds nearby and confirms the door with you by phone or WhatsApp once you have your bags.

Meet and greet puts the chauffeur inside the hall with a name sign and hands on the luggage, as an add-on plus any airport parking charge. Arrivals are tracked live and the pickup slides with the aircraft, so a late inbound does not cost you the car or the rate. Domestic arrivals include 45 minutes of complimentary waiting and international arrivals 60 minutes, measured from touchdown, and every other pickup carries a 15 minute grace period.

Routes off the field

For the east side of Manhattan the run is the Grand Central Parkway west to the Robert F. Kennedy Bridge and then straight down the FDR Drive. When the bridge is heavy the alternative is the Brooklyn-Queens Expressway to the Long Island Expressway and the Queens-Midtown Tunnel, and the Ed Koch Queensboro Bridge is the third option, landing at Queens Plaza on the way through.

The Upper East Side is the shortest Manhattan trip of all from here, off the FDR at the 96th Street exit. The Upper West Side adds either the 96th Street transverse through Central Park or a run up the Harlem River Drive. The Financial District is the FDR the whole way south to the Battery. Brooklyn leaves the Grand Central Parkway for the BQE over the Kosciuszko Bridge. The Bronx is the Robert F. Kennedy Bridge, or the Whitestone Expressway and the Bronx-Whitestone Bridge from the Flushing side. Astoria and Long Island City are minutes away on Astoria Boulevard and 21st Street.

The field's own approaches, and Flushing next door

Everything that reaches LaGuardia by road comes off the Grand Central Parkway, and the parkway's junctions on either side set the pattern for the day. West of the field it meets the Brooklyn-Queens Expressway near Astoria Park and the Hell Gate Bridge before running on to the Robert F. Kennedy Bridge toll plaza. East of the field it meets the Whitestone Expressway beside Flushing Bay, and immediately past that junction sit Citi Field, Flushing Meadows Corona Park and the tennis center. From late August into September that neighboring traffic and the airport's own traffic share the same ramps.

On the ground the useful streets are few. Marine Terminal Road serves the Marine Air Terminal at the western end of the site, off the parkway exit for Astoria Boulevard and 82nd Street, and those two streets connect the whole field to the Jackson Heights and East Elmhurst grid. The free waiting lot for drivers is on 94th Street between 23rd Avenue and Ditmars Boulevard. Northern Boulevard and Roosevelt Avenue run parallel further south and are the surface alternative when the parkway stops moving. A car held on any of them is a short repositioning rather than a fresh dispatch, which is what lets it come forward quickly when an aircraft lands early.

Points to fix at the time of booking

Curbside or inside the terminal?

Either. Curbside is quicker when you are traveling light and know the building, and the chauffeur confirms the door by phone or WhatsApp as you come down. Meet and greet is the better choice with luggage, with children, or on a first visit, and is an add-on plus any airport parking charge. Both are set at the time of booking rather than negotiated on the day.

Which LaGuardia terminals do you serve?

Terminals B and C, which handle every scheduled flight. Departures are dropped at the terminal letter you give when booking. Arrivals are met at the curb or the designated pickup area of that terminal, or inside the hall with a name sign if you have added meet and greet.

How long is the trip into Midtown?

The road distance is about eight or nine miles, which is the shortest of any commercial airport into the middle of Manhattan. The elapsed time is entirely a function of the Grand Central Parkway and the crossing you use, so no time is promised. What is fixed is the fare, because the LaGuardia transfer is a flat rate rather than a metered ride.

Is there a train to LaGuardia?

No. There is no rail link into the terminals. The free Q70 LaGuardia Link bus runs from the Jackson Heights-Roosevelt Avenue and 74 Street-Broadway subway stations to Terminals B and C, and every other arrival leaves by road. The car fare stays flat whichever direction you travel.
