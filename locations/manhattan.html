Manhattan, New York

Chauffeur and black car service in Manhattan

Flat rates of $165 to JFK, Newark Liberty and Teterboro and $130 to LaGuardia, from any address between Inwood and Battery Park City.

An island two miles wide and thirteen long

Manhattan runs 13.4 miles from Inwood at the north tip to the Battery at the south, and about 2.3 miles across at its widest, so the practical distance between a pickup in Washington Heights and a pickup in Battery Park City is the whole length of the borough. Above 14th Street the grid does most of the work for a driver. Numbered streets run east to west, avenues run north to south, and a cross street locates a building faster than its number does.

Below 14th Street the grid stops. In the West Village, SoHo, TriBeCa and the Financial District the streets are named and angled. Greenwich Street and Greenwich Avenue are two different roads. West 4th Street crosses West 10th, 11th and 12th. Giving the nearest corner alongside the address removes the guesswork before the car is anywhere near you.

Every airport run starts with a river crossing

Manhattan has no airport on it, so the route out is chosen by where the pickup sits. From Midtown, JFK is roughly 15 road miles by way of the Queens-Midtown Tunnel, the Long Island Expressway and the Van Wyck Expressway. From Lower Manhattan the same destination is usually approached through the Hugh L. Carey Tunnel and along the Belt Parkway instead. LaGuardia is the nearest commercial field at roughly 9 miles, reached over the Ed Koch Queensboro Bridge or the Robert F. Kennedy Bridge and then the Grand Central Parkway. Newark Liberty lies west, through the Lincoln Tunnel or the Holland Tunnel and down the New Jersey Turnpike. Teterboro, the general aviation field in Bergen County, is reached by the Lincoln Tunnel and Route 3, or over the George Washington Bridge.

Where the work actually clusters

Weekday demand concentrates in Midtown. The office corridor runs along Park, Madison and Lexington Avenues between 42nd and 59th Street. Grand Central Terminal sits at 42nd and Park. Moynihan Train Hall faces Ninth Avenue between 31st and 33rd. The Javits Center runs along Eleventh Avenue north of 34th. Evening work moves west and north instead: the theatre blocks between 41st and 54th Street empty quickly once the curtain comes down, Carnegie Hall stands at 57th and Seventh, and Lincoln Center sits on Columbus Avenue at 65th.

Uptown, Museum Mile runs up Fifth Avenue from the Metropolitan Museum at 82nd Street to 105th, with the hotels along the Central Park border a block or two away. Downtown keeps its own set of approaches, and each one behaves differently. Wall Street and Broad Street are narrow and often restricted. Brookfield Place and the South Street Seaport have separate drop points. The cruise piers on Twelfth Avenue between 46th and 54th Street load and unload on a schedule set by the ship, not by traffic.

Four Manhattan neighborhoods have their own page because what they ask for is not the same. SoHo is mostly evening work around the Broadway and Prince Street blocks and the restaurants below Houston Street. The Financial District is early and weekday. The Upper East Side runs residential and appointment driven, between Fifth Avenue and the East River from 59th Street to 96th. The Upper West Side is Lincoln Center, Columbus Avenue and Central Park West.

What to expect

Four steps, in the order they happen.

  1. Give the street number and the two nearest cross streets, and say which entrance to use. Park Avenue between 40th and 46th Street runs on a viaduct around Grand Central Terminal with a street level roadway underneath it, so those two curbs are not interchangeable.
  2. Book at least 24 hours ahead where you can. That secures availability and vehicle choice. Same-day requests are accepted when possible.
  3. For an airport arrival, flights are monitored in real time and the pickup time is adjusted if the flight is delayed. Domestic arrivals include 45 minutes of complimentary waiting, international arrivals 60 minutes, and every other pickup carries a 15 minute grace period.
  4. Add meet and greet if you want the chauffeur waiting inside the terminal with a name sign and help with the bags. It costs $25 plus any airport parking charge. At JFK the pickup is made inside the terminal near baggage claim.

Hourly on the island

Three stops or more

A Manhattan day with three or more stops usually costs less and runs calmer on an hourly booking than on separate one-way rides, because the vehicle stays with you between stops instead of being released and re-dispatched. Manhattan questions

Four things worth knowing

Which parts of Manhattan do you cover?

The whole island. That runs from Inwood and Washington Heights down through Harlem, Morningside Heights, the Upper West and Upper East Sides, Midtown, Chelsea, the Flatiron, Greenwich Village, SoHo and TriBeCa to the Financial District and Battery Park City.

Four of those areas have a page of their own: SoHo, Financial District, Upper East Side and Upper West Side.

What address detail helps most in Manhattan?

The street number, the two nearest cross streets, and the entrance. Two cases cause most of the confusion in this borough. Park Avenue between 40th and 46th Street splits into a viaduct around Grand Central Terminal and a roadway at street level, which are separate curbs reached in different ways. And south of 14th Street the named grid produces genuine duplicates, Greenwich Street and Greenwich Avenue being the best known pair.

How do the airport rates work from Manhattan?

Business luxury SUV airport rates are $165 for JFK, Newark and Teterboro, and $130 for LaGuardia. They are flat rates rather than meter readings, so traffic on the Van Wyck does not change the figure. All prices exclude taxes, tolls & gratuity, and the final total is confirmed at booking. Meet and greet inside the terminal adds $25 plus any applicable airport parking charge.

Hourly service or separate one-way rides?

For a single trip from a Manhattan address to one airport, the flat rate is the cheaper option. For a day with three or more stops on the island, hourly service is normally better value and always less coordination, because the same vehicle waits with you between legs. Hourly starts at $85 an hour depending on the vehicle class, with a three hour minimum.
