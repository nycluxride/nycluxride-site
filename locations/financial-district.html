Chauffeur service in the Financial District

Flat rates to Newark Liberty, JFK, Teterboro and LaGuardia, from Wall Street and Broad Street across to Battery Park City and the Seaport.

A street plan that predates the grid

The Financial District fills the bottom of Manhattan, roughly from Chambers and Fulton Streets down to the Battery, with the Hudson on one flank and the East River on the other. None of it follows the numbered grid laid out uptown. Wall Street, Pine Street, Cedar Street, Maiden Lane, Beaver Street, Stone Street and Exchange Place are short, angled and mostly one way, and several are narrow enough that one stopped vehicle holds up the whole block. Broadway and Water Street are the two continuous routes through the middle. West Street carries the Hudson side and the FDR Drive the East River side.

The security arrangement around the New York Stock Exchange at Broad and Wall closes several of those blocks to general traffic behind bollards and checkpoints, so a Wall Street address is often served from William Street, Water Street or Broadway rather than from Wall Street itself. Towers down here also tend to have a side street entrance that works better than the address on the letterhead. Naming the one you want is worth several minutes at either end of the trip.

Newark is the quiet advantage of a downtown pickup

West Street runs north from Battery Place directly to the Holland Tunnel entrance at Canal Street, and from the New Jersey end the Turnpike goes straight to Newark Liberty. That makes Newark a shorter road trip from Wall Street than it is from most of Midtown, which is the opposite of what people expect. JFK is reached through the Battery Park Underpass and the Hugh L. Carey Tunnel, then the Gowanus Expressway and the Belt Parkway, or over the Brooklyn Bridge onto the BQE.

LaGuardia is the longest of the three from this end of the island, up the FDR Drive to the Robert F. Kennedy Bridge and then the Grand Central Parkway. Teterboro follows the Holland Tunnel and then runs north and west into Bergen County. Time of day decides more than distance here. Between roughly four and seven on a weekday the approaches to both tunnels queue, and the Brooklyn Bridge ramp at Park Row queues with them, which is one reason so much of the work in this area is booked before seven in the morning.

The blocks the work actually comes from

Weekday demand clusters in a small area. The Stock Exchange and Federal Hall sit at Broad and Wall, the Federal Reserve Bank of New York is on Liberty Street, and Trinity Church closes the west end of Wall Street at Broadway. One World Trade Center, the Oculus and the 9/11 Memorial occupy the blocks between Church Street and West Street. Brookfield Place faces the Hudson at Vesey Street with its own drop-off away from the tower entrances. Battery Park City and Rockefeller Park run north along the water from Battery Place.

Whitehall Terminal at South Ferry handles the Staten Island Ferry, which carries passengers only, so a car cannot cross with you and a separate vehicle is needed on the far side. Pier 11 at Wall Street is the East River ferry landing. South Street Seaport and Pier 17 fill up in the evening, and Stone Street's restaurant row does the same in a space too narrow for a car to wait in. Bowling Green, 28 Liberty and One Liberty Plaza round out the addresses that come up most often.

The lanes north of Wall Street

Between Wall Street and Chambers Street the plan tightens again. Nassau Street, John Street, Cortlandt Street, Dey Street and Maiden Lane are short and partly closed to vehicles, with Fulton Center at the middle of them as the transit interchange. Rector Street, Trinity Place and Greenwich Street run north to south on the western side, and Murray Street, Warren Street and Barclay Street mark the top edge near Chambers. North End Avenue serves the residential blocks of Battery Park City above Vesey Street.

On the eastern side the old shoreline still shows in the names. Front Street, South Street, Old Slip and Coenties Slip are named for water that is no longer there, and Hanover Square and Fraunces Tavern sit among them. The Battery Maritime Building beside Whitehall Terminal handles the Governors Island crossing. Because so many of these lanes are short and one way, a driver given only a street name and a number will often approach from the wrong end, and the remedy is always the same: name the nearest corner.

Asked downtown

Practical detail below Chambers Street

Can a car reach my Wall Street entrance?

Sometimes, and it depends on the block. The streets closest to the Stock Exchange are behind fixed barriers and checkpoints, and several nearby lanes are too narrow to wait in. The practical answer is to name a corner one block clear of the tower, on William Street, Water Street or Broadway, and to say which entrance you are coming out of so the chauffeur is standing in the right place.

Why book an early departure from downtown?

Because both of the tunnels that serve this area queue in the afternoon. A Newark flight leaving in the evening means the Holland Tunnel approach at its worst, while the same trip before seven in the morning is a clear run up West Street. For arrivals, flights are tracked in real time and the pickup is moved if the aircraft is late, so an early booking does not cost you if the schedule slips.

Do you meet the ferry terminals down here?

Yes. Whitehall Terminal at South Ferry and Pier 11 at Wall Street are both regular pickup points. The Staten Island Ferry takes foot passengers only, so a trip that continues across the harbor needs a car on each side, or a single vehicle over the Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge instead. Pickups on the far side are covered under Staten Island.

How far does the Financial District coverage reach?

From the Battery north to Chambers Street and Fulton Street, taking in Wall Street, Broad Street, Water Street, Pearl Street, the World Trade Center blocks, Battery Park City, Bowling Green, Whitehall Street and the South Street Seaport. Immediately north of that boundary the coverage continues into SoHo and across the rest of Manhattan.
