Chauffeur service in Brooklyn

Flat rates to JFK, Newark Liberty, Teterboro and LaGuardia, from Greenpoint and Williamsburg down to Bay Ridge, Sheepshead Bay and Coney Island.

The western end of Long Island, and every exit but Queens crosses water

Brooklyn sits on the western tip of Long Island, so any trip out of the borough that is not headed into Queens has to cross something. Three bridges and one tunnel reach Manhattan. The Brooklyn Bridge and the Manhattan Bridge lift off from Downtown Brooklyn and DUMBO, the Williamsburg Bridge crosses from the north end, and the Hugh L. Carey Tunnel runs under the harbor from Red Hook and the Gowanus. The Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge is the only road link to Staten Island. Queens is the one neighbor reached over land. The Pulaski and Kosciuszko bridges cross Newtown Creek in the north, and farther south the border is a line on land, crossed by the Jackie Robinson Parkway and by avenues such as Myrtle, Atlantic and Jamaica.

Inside the borough the distances are real. Greenpoint to Sea Gate is close to fifteen road miles, and a pickup in Williamsburg has almost nothing in common with a pickup in Marine Park. Naming the neighborhood as well as the street removes the guesswork, because Brooklyn repeats its street names freely and has a Third Avenue, a Third Street and a Third Place within a mile of each other around Gowanus and Carroll Gardens.

The BQE is the spine and the bottleneck

The Brooklyn-Queens Expressway runs the length of the borough from the Kosciuszko Bridge down through the Gowanus Expressway to the Verrazzano. It carries most of the through traffic, and the narrow section below Brooklyn Heights slows badly through the afternoon. The Belt Parkway is the alternative along the southern shore, and the Prospect Expressway cuts up from Church Avenue toward the Gowanus. Because the parkways carry commercial vehicle and height restrictions, a Sprinter booking is routed on the expressways instead of the Belt.

Surface streets carry more of the load here than they do in Manhattan. Atlantic Avenue crosses the borough east to west. Flatbush Avenue runs from the foot of the Manhattan Bridge all the way to Marine Park. Eastern Parkway and Ocean Parkway are wide and heavily signaled. In DUMBO, Water Street and Washington Street are laid in Belgian block under the bridge approaches, which is picturesque and slow, so a car will usually wait on a smoother corner nearby if you ask for one.

JFK is the near field for most Brooklyn addresses

From south and central Brooklyn the airport run is the Belt Parkway east to the JFK Expressway, or Atlantic Avenue out to Conduit Boulevard. From Williamsburg and Greenpoint it is usually the BQE to the Long Island Expressway and then the Van Wyck Expressway, or the Jackie Robinson Parkway across the middle of Queens. LaGuardia lies north through Queens by way of the BQE and the Grand Central Parkway.

Newark Liberty is a southwest trip, over the Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge, along the Staten Island Expressway and across the Goethals Bridge to the New Jersey Turnpike. When the bridge is heavy the alternative is the Carey Tunnel and then the Holland Tunnel. Teterboro uses either of those two starts and then runs north into Bergen County.

Venues, offices and the cruise berth

Barclays Center stands at the junction of Atlantic and Flatbush Avenues, above the busiest transit interchange in the borough, and on event nights the surrounding blocks are the slowest in Brooklyn. The Brooklyn Academy of Music is a few streets east on Lafayette Avenue, and Kings Theatre sits further out on Flatbush Avenue in Flatbush. The Brooklyn Cruise Terminal is at Red Hook, reached from Van Brunt Street and Bowne Street, and it loads and unloads on the ship's schedule rather than the road's. The cruise terminal guide covers embarkation day at Red Hook and at the Manhattan piers.

Weekday work concentrates in Downtown Brooklyn around Court Street, Livingston Street and the MetroTech blocks, in the Brooklyn Navy Yard, and at Industry City in Sunset Park. Evening and weekend work runs to the restaurants of Williamsburg and Carroll Gardens, the event spaces along Kent Avenue, the Brooklyn Museum and Brooklyn Botanic Garden on Eastern Parkway, and in summer the Coney Island boardwalk and Brighton Beach.

South of Prospect Park, and the long streets that get you there

Below Prospect Park the borough opens into a set of large residential neighborhoods that visitors rarely see. Windsor Terrace and Kensington sit against the park, Borough Park and Midwood fill the middle, and Bensonhurst, Dyker Heights, Gravesend and Sheepshead Bay reach the water. Canarsie, Mill Basin and Marine Park hold the southeastern shore beside the Belt Parkway. Green-Wood Cemetery covers a large block between Sunset Park and Windsor Terrace, and Kings Highway, Nostrand Avenue, Utica Avenue and Linden Boulevard are the long surface routes through all of it.

The northern end is the opposite: dense, short-blocked and crowded on foot. Brooklyn Bridge Park runs beneath both bridges from Atlantic Avenue up to the Manhattan Bridge, with the Brooklyn Heights Promenade above it. Atlantic Terminal and Borough Hall put the borough's transit and civic addresses within a few streets of each other. Boerum Hill, Cobble Hill and Clinton Hill are brownstone blocks with narrow curbs and alternate side rules that leave almost no room for a vehicle to wait, which is why a named corner works better here than a house number does.

Common Brooklyn queries

Details that decide a Brooklyn trip

Is JFK really the closest airport from here?

For most of the borough, yes. Anything from Park Slope south and east is closer to JFK than to LaGuardia, and the Belt Parkway leads almost to the terminal ring. From Greenpoint and Williamsburg the two are much closer in road distance, and on a bad afternoon the BQE decides which one is quicker. Newark Liberty is the outlier, since it needs the Verrazzano or a pair of tunnels. Road by road detail is under airport routes from Brooklyn and Queens.

Where should a Barclays Center pickup be arranged?

At a corner a few blocks clear of Atlantic and Flatbush Avenues, agreed when you book. On event nights the blocks around the arena are the slowest in the borough, and a car waiting inside them cannot move until the crowd has gone.

Why name a corner rather than a house number?

Because Brooklyn repeats its street names, with a Third Avenue, a Third Street and a Third Place within a mile of each other around Gowanus and Carroll Gardens, and because the brownstone blocks of Boerum Hill, Cobble Hill and Clinton Hill have narrow curbs and alternate side rules that leave almost no room for a car to wait. A named corner settles both.

Which parts of Brooklyn do you cover?

The whole borough. That runs from Greenpoint, Williamsburg and Bushwick in the north through DUMBO, Brooklyn Heights, Downtown Brooklyn, Fort Greene, Carroll Gardens, Gowanus and Park Slope, out to Bedford-Stuyvesant, Crown Heights and East New York, and down through Sunset Park, Bay Ridge, Borough Park, Flatbush and Midwood to Sheepshead Bay, Brighton Beach and Coney Island.
