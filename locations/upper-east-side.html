Chauffeur service on the Upper East Side

LaGuardia is roughly eight miles up the FDR Drive, and every airport is on a flat rate from 59th Street to 96th between Fifth Avenue and the East River.

Fifth Avenue to the East River, 59th Street to 96th

The Upper East Side runs north from 59th Street to 96th Street and east from Fifth Avenue along Central Park to the river. Lenox Hill fills the sixties and seventies, Carnegie Hill takes the upper eighties and nineties nearest the park, and Yorkville covers the blocks east of Third Avenue in the eighties and nineties. Sutton Place sits just below the southern boundary.

The avenues are one way and that decides how a car reaches a door. Fifth Avenue runs south along the park, Madison Avenue runs north, Park Avenue carries both directions with a planted median that limits left turns, Lexington runs south and Third runs north. Two addresses one block apart on Fifth and Madison are approached from opposite ends of the neighborhood, so naming the avenue with the cross street removes a full loop of the block. Most buildings here are doorman buildings and the pickup is made at the canopy, which is worth saying if yours has more than one.

The closest Manhattan neighborhood to LaGuardia

The FDR Drive is the spine of this side of the island, and the Robert F. Kennedy Bridge lifts it straight onto the Grand Central Parkway. That puts LaGuardia around eight road miles from a Lenox Hill or Yorkville pickup and explains why so much of the airport work here goes to LGA rather than to JFK. Access to the FDR is from the sixties and from 96th Street, so the entrance chosen depends on your cross street.

JFK is either the Queens-Midtown Tunnel or the Ed Koch Queensboro Bridge at 59th Street, then the Long Island Expressway and the Van Wyck Expressway. Westbound is the harder direction from here. Newark Liberty and Teterboro both begin with a crossing of the island, on the 79th Street or 86th Street transverse through Central Park to the West Side Highway and the Lincoln Tunnel, or by the 96th Street crosstown to the Henry Hudson Parkway and the George Washington Bridge. On a weekday morning the transverse is the slow part of that journey rather than the tunnel.

Museum Mile and the gala evenings

Fifth Avenue above 82nd Street is Museum Mile. The Metropolitan Museum of Art holds 82nd Street, the Neue Galerie 86th, the Guggenheim 89th, the Cooper Hewitt 91st and the Jewish Museum 92nd, with the Frick Collection further south at 70th Street. A benefit evening on that stretch puts several hundred cars on a one-way avenue with the park on one side and no cross traffic to absorb them. A set-down on Madison Avenue with a walk through the cross street is consistently faster than joining the queue on Fifth.

The rest of the cultural work sits inland. The Asia Society is at Park Avenue and 70th Street, Hunter College at Lexington and 68th, the 92nd Street Y at Lexington and 92nd, and Bloomingdale's at 59th and Lexington. Carl Schurz Park and Gracie Mansion close the eastern end at East End Avenue and 88th Street, and the Roosevelt Island Tramway leaves from Second Avenue at 59th.

The York Avenue hospital corridor

Between roughly 66th and 71st Streets, York Avenue carries one of the densest concentrations of medical addresses in the country. NewYork-Presbyterian and Weill Cornell Medicine, Memorial Sloan Kettering and Rockefeller University all stand within a few blocks of each other, and Lenox Hill Hospital is further west near 77th Street. Each of them has its own driveway or ambulance bay rather than a simple street entrance, so the building name and the department matter more than the house number when a car is meeting a patient.

Appointment work here is quiet, repeat and time-sensitive. A morning consultation followed by a wait and a return home is normally booked hourly rather than as two separate rides, because the chauffeur holds the car nearby instead of you calling for a second one at an unknown time.

East of Third Avenue, and the water's edge

The blocks east of Third Avenue read differently from the ones along the park. First Avenue and Second Avenue are commercial and busy, York Avenue and East End Avenue are quieter, and the East River Esplanade runs behind them above the FDR Drive, with John Jay Park at 77th Street and Cherokee Place beside it. Sotheby's stands on York Avenue at 72nd Street. The FDR is entered from the low sixties or from 96th Street, so a Yorkville pickup in the eighties joins it in one direction or the other rather than straight across.

At the southern end the Ed Koch Queensboro Bridge has two roadways, and its Manhattan ramps land at 59th and 60th Streets among the Bloomingdale's blocks, which is the busiest corner in the district on a weekday evening. Temple Emanu-El stands a few streets north at Fifth Avenue and 65th, and the Central Park Zoo is inside the park at 64th. At the far end of the neighborhood the Mount Sinai campus begins just above 96th Street on Fifth Avenue, reached the same way.

Appointments and standing bookings

The same car at the same time each week

A great deal of the work on these blocks repeats. A weekly appointment on York Avenue, a school run on the same two cross streets, a Thursday evening at a Fifth Avenue museum. Those are easier to hold as a standing booking than to re-enter each time, and a corporate or household account can be billed monthly. Every vehicle class books by the hour for hourly chauffeur work, with a three hour minimum. See hourly rates by class.

Asked between 59th and 96th

Detail that matters on a one-way avenue

Can you collect from a hospital entrance?

Yes. The York Avenue institutions each have their own driveway and more than one door, so give the building name and the department rather than the street number and the chauffeur will be at the right one. For an appointment with an uncertain finishing time, hourly service is the better arrangement, because the car stays nearby instead of being called back from elsewhere.

What address detail helps most on these blocks?

The avenue, the cross street and the canopy. The avenues run one way here, so a Fifth Avenue door and a Madison Avenue door on the same block are reached from different directions. Park Avenue has a median that blocks most left turns. And on the long blocks between Fifth and Lexington a building can be closer to the far cross street than to the one it is numbered from.

Which airport is quickest from here?

LaGuardia, on road distance, at about eight miles by the FDR Drive and the Robert F. Kennedy Bridge, and it also carries the lower flat rate. JFK goes through Queens by the Queensboro Bridge or the Midtown Tunnel. Newark Liberty and Teterboro both start with a crossing of Central Park, which is the part of the trip that varies most.

Where does the Upper East Side start and stop?

59th Street to 96th Street, Fifth Avenue to the East River, which takes in Lenox Hill, Carnegie Hill, Yorkville and the Museum Mile frontage. Above 96th Street and below 59th the coverage continues across the rest of Manhattan, and the far side of Central Park is the Upper West Side.
