Chauffeur service in SoHo

Evening pickups on the cast-iron blocks between Houston Street and Canal Street, with flat rates to Newark Liberty, JFK, Teterboro and LaGuardia.

Cast iron between Houston Street and Canal Street

SoHo takes its name from sitting south of Houston Street. Canal Street closes it at the bottom, Lafayette Street and Crosby Street bound the east side, and Sixth Avenue and West Broadway the west. Almost all of it falls inside the SoHo-Cast Iron Historic District, which is why the buildings resemble one another and why the streets behave nothing like the numbered grid uptown. Greene Street, Mercer Street, Wooster Street and Crosby Street are narrow one-way lanes still surfaced in Belgian block. Broadway is the wide one, and Prince Street, Spring Street, Broome Street and Grand Street run across.

The most common mix-up in the neighborhood is that West Broadway is a different street from Broadway and runs four short blocks west of it, past Mercer, Greene and Wooster Streets. The second most common is a repeated number: 100 Greene Street and 100 Wooster Street are one block apart and look nearly identical from a moving car. Both are solved by giving the cross street with the number, which is why every booking in this area asks for one.

The Holland Tunnel is the neighbor nobody mentions

The Holland Tunnel entrance sits at Canal Street and Varick Street on SoHo's southwest corner. That makes Newark Liberty and the whole New Jersey side unusually quick from here, and it makes weekday evenings unusually slow. From the middle of the afternoon the tunnel queue backs along Canal Street and into the streets that feed it, and on a Friday it starts earlier. A pickup on Thompson Street at five in the afternoon is a different task from the same pickup at ten in the morning, and the route out is chosen accordingly.

In the other direction JFK is reached across the Manhattan Bridge onto the Brooklyn-Queens Expressway and the Belt Parkway, or down West Street to the Hugh L. Carey Tunnel. LaGuardia goes north on Houston Street to the FDR Drive, then the Robert F. Kennedy Bridge and the Grand Central Parkway, with the Williamsburg Bridge and the BQE as the alternative. Teterboro uses the Holland Tunnel and then heads north, or the West Side Highway up to the Lincoln Tunnel.

An evening and weekend neighborhood

Chauffeured work here is concentrated after dark and on weekends. The restaurants below Houston Street, the galleries on Wooster Street and Greene Street, the flagship stores along Broadway and Prince Street, and the showroom appointments that fill the calendar during the fashion weeks make up most of it. The hotels sit on West Broadway, Spring Street, Crosby Street and Mercer Street, each with a frontage narrow enough that a waiting car is noticed.

For a driver the useful landmarks are the ones you can see from the road. The Little Singer Building stands on Broadway near Prince Street. The Haughwout Building holds the corner of Broadway and Broome Street. The Angelika Film Center is at Houston Street and Mercer Street. The Canal Street tunnel entrance is the reference point for everything at the southern end.

Where a car can actually stand

On the cast-iron blocks a vehicle cannot idle. Greene Street and Mercer Street are effectively one lane wide with a delivery truck in it for much of the day, and the curb elsewhere is taken by loading zones and hydrants. The workable arrangement is a corner on one of the wider streets: West Broadway, Sixth Avenue, Lafayette Street, or Houston Street itself. Thirty seconds of walking replaces ten minutes of a car circling the block.

Weekend afternoons add pedestrian volume that the roadway cannot absorb. The corners of Broadway with Prince Street and Spring Street are the busiest, and a pickup one block east on Crosby Street or one block west on Wooster Street clears faster even though it looks further away on a map.

The edges: Hudson Square, Nolita and the streets below Grand

SoHo's usefulness to a driver often lies just outside it. Hudson Square, west of Sixth Avenue around Varick Street and King Street, is where the Holland Tunnel traffic actually forms, and Charlton Street, Vandam Street and Watts Street feed into it. Nolita, immediately east across Lafayette Street, holds the Puck Building at the corner of Lafayette and Houston and a set of narrow streets around Mulberry and Elizabeth that behave exactly as SoHo's do. TriBeCa begins below Canal Street and is a separate proposition again.

Two naming quirks catch people out here. Sixth Avenue is also signed as Avenue of the Americas, so a car looking for one is looking for the other. And Howard Street, Grand Street and Broome Street all run east to west within three blocks of each other at the southern end of the neighborhood, with Grand carrying on across the Bowery beyond it. Neither of those matters if the booking carries a cross street.

Asked below Houston Street

Local detail that saves a circuit of the block

Can the car wait outside my door?

On Greene, Mercer, Wooster and Crosby Streets, usually not for long. They are one lane wide, cobbled, and busy with deliveries. The reliable arrangement is a corner on West Broadway, Sixth Avenue, Lafayette Street or Houston Street, agreed when you book. Every non-airport pickup carries a 15 minute grace period, so there is room for a short walk without the clock working against you.

Is Newark really quicker from SoHo?

In road distance, yes, because the Holland Tunnel entrance is on the neighborhood's own corner at Canal Street and Varick Street. In elapsed time it depends entirely on the hour. The same portal that makes the trip short in the morning is the reason Canal Street stops moving in the late afternoon. No travel time is promised either way, and the flat rate does not change with the traffic.

Which street names get confused in SoHo?

Three cause most of the trouble. West Broadway is a different street from Broadway and runs four short blocks west of it, past Mercer, Greene and Wooster Streets. Sixth Avenue is also signed as Avenue of the Americas. And Howard Street, Grand Street and Broome Street all run east to west within three blocks of each other at the southern end. A cross street given with the number clears up all three.

Where does SoHo start and stop for a booking?

Houston Street at the top, Canal Street at the bottom, Lafayette Street and Crosby Street on the east, Sixth Avenue and West Broadway on the west. Nolita and the Bowery immediately east, TriBeCa below Canal Street and Greenwich Village above Houston Street fall under Manhattan coverage, and the blocks below Chambers Street under the Financial District.
