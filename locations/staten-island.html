Chauffeur service on Staten Island

Newark Liberty is the near field, and Newark, LaGuardia and JFK are each on a flat rate, from St. George and the North Shore down to Tottenville.

Four bridges and a passenger-only ferry

Staten Island has exactly one road link to the rest of New York City. The Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge crosses the Narrows to Bay Ridge in Brooklyn. Three further bridges go to New Jersey instead: the Goethals Bridge to Elizabeth, the Outerbridge Crossing to Perth Amboy and the Bayonne Bridge to Bayonne. The Staten Island Ferry runs between the St. George terminal and Whitehall Terminal in Lower Manhattan, but it carries foot passengers only, so a car cannot cross on the boat and a journey that uses it needs a vehicle waiting on the far side.

That single Brooklyn crossing shapes every schedule made here. Everything eastbound funnels onto one bridge, and when the Verrazzano is slow the only alternative runs the wrong way, through New Jersey. It is the reason bookings on this side of the harbor are made earlier than the mileage suggests they need to be.

Newark is the near airport, which is unusual for a New York borough

For most Staten Island addresses Newark Liberty is the closest of the four airports, reached over the Goethals Bridge and down the New Jersey Turnpike. From the North Shore that is a run of little more than ten miles. Teterboro is the same start and then north on the Turnpike, or over the Bayonne Bridge. JFK is the long way round, east over the Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge and along the Belt Parkway, closer to twenty-five road miles from the middle of the island. LaGuardia adds the Brooklyn-Queens Expressway and the Grand Central Parkway on top of that same bridge. The comparison of JFK, LaGuardia and Newark sets out how the three fields differ.

The flat rates do not follow those distances, which works in your favor here. Newark and Teterboro carry the same flat rate as JFK, and the fare holds whether the Verrazzano is moving or not. Tolls on the crossings are not part of the quoted rate. They are billed at cost and charged after the ride to the card saved at booking.

Three roads across, and everything else is local

The Staten Island Expressway is the only continuous route across the northern half of the island, running from the Verrazzano toll plaza west to the Goethals Bridge. The West Shore Expressway, signed as Route 440, drops south from it toward the Outerbridge Crossing, and the Korean War Veterans Parkway carries on from there into the South Shore. Everything else is surface road. Hylan Boulevard runs almost the full length of the eastern side and is long and heavily signaled. Victory Boulevard, Richmond Avenue, Forest Avenue, Amboy Road and Arthur Kill Road carry most of the rest.

Distances inside the borough are real. Tottenville to St. George is close to fifteen miles on local roads, so a six in the morning flight from the South Shore has its lead time measured from Hylan Boulevard rather than from the bridge. Round trips are common for the same reason, and booking both legs at once holds the same vehicle class for the return.

North Shore, the hills and the South Shore

The North Shore holds St. George, Tompkinsville, Stapleton and Port Richmond, with the ferry terminal, Empire Outlets and the Snug Harbor Cultural Center inside a few minutes of each other. Grymes Hill and Todt Hill rise behind them, and Todt Hill is the highest ground in the city, which in practice means steep private drives and houses set well back from the road where a house number is not visible from a car.

The middle of the island runs through Bulls Head, New Springville and Willowbrook, where the College of Staten Island and Staten Island University Hospital generate weekday work. The South Shore carries Great Kills, Eltingville, Annadale, Huguenot, Prince's Bay and Tottenville, largely residential and largely early departures. Historic Richmond Town, the Staten Island Zoo, Fort Wadsworth under the bridge tower and Great Kills Park are the visitor addresses, and Wagner College sits on Grymes Hill.

The North Shore road, and the railway down the length of it

Richmond Terrace follows the Kill Van Kull along the whole northern edge, from St. George past Snug Harbor to Mariners Harbor, with the Bayonne Bridge lifting off it midway. Bay Street runs the other way, south from the ferry terminal through Tompkinsville and Stapleton to Rosebank and Fort Wadsworth. Clove Road and Slosson Avenue climb between the two over the ridge. The Alice Austen House and the Staten Island Museum are on this side, along with most of the borough's older housing.

The Staten Island Railway runs from St. George all the way to Tottenville, roughly parallel to Hylan Boulevard and Amboy Road, and its station names double as a useful shorthand for a South Shore address. Inland, Freshkills Park now covers the ground west of Richmond Avenue between the West Shore Expressway and Travis. Silver Lake, Westerleigh, Castleton Corners and Emerson Hill fill the center of the island. Conference House Park holds the southern tip at Tottenville, and Mount Loretto the bluffs above Prince's Bay.

Early departures and family travel

Two legs booked as one

Most airport work from this borough is a pair of trips rather than one, because a family flying out on a Friday is a family coming back on a Sunday. Booking both legs together holds the same vehicle class for the return and puts the arrival on flight tracking, so a delayed inbound does not leave anyone waiting at the Newark curb. A Business SUV takes five passengers with three large and three carry-on bags. The twelve seat Sprinter takes ten large bags. Child seats are supplied at no extra charge.

Asked on the island

What an island booking has to account for

Is Newark really closer than JFK from here?

Yes, for almost the whole island. Newark Liberty is over the Goethals Bridge and down the Turnpike, while JFK means crossing the Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge and then most of the width of Brooklyn. The flat rate is the same either way, so the choice is about your flight rather than your fare. LaGuardia carries the lower flat rate and uses the same Verrazzano crossing as JFK.

Can the car come across on the ferry?

No. The Staten Island Ferry takes foot passengers only. A trip that uses the boat needs one car to the St. George terminal and a second one waiting at Whitehall Terminal in Lower Manhattan, which is straightforward to arrange. The alternative is a single vehicle over the Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge for the whole journey, which is usually the simpler option with luggage.

Is the Verrazzano toll charged both ways?

Yes. The Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge collects tolls in both directions. Tolls are not part of the quoted rate. They are billed at cost and charged after the ride to the card saved at booking, so a round trip over the bridge carries two of them.

How early should an airport departure be booked?

Earlier than the distance suggests, particularly from the South Shore, because Hylan Boulevard and the single Verrazzano crossing both add time that a map does not show. No travel time is promised. What is fixed is the rate and the vehicle, and booking at least 24 hours ahead secures both. Same-day requests are taken when a car is available.
