JFK, LaGuardia or Newark: how New York's three airports differ

April 17, 2026, updated September 28, 2026

Each of New York's three commercial airports changes the trip in its own way: the roads that reach it, the terminal you use, where a car can meet you and how early to leave.

Three airports, three different trips

JFK sits in Jamaica, Queens, on the northern shore of Jamaica Bay. Cars reach it from the north on the Van Wyck Expressway (I-678) and from the west on the JFK Expressway off the Belt Parkway, and its five passenger terminals, numbered 1, 4, 5, 7 and 8, stand around a single loop road joined by the AirTrain. As of September 2026, Terminal 1 serves international airlines including Air France, Lufthansa, Korean Air and Turkish Airlines. Terminal 4 is Delta, with Virgin Atlantic, Emirates and others. Terminal 5 is JetBlue. Terminal 7 carries Aer Lingus, Air Canada, ANA and Frontier, and Terminal 8 is American Airlines with British Airways, Iberia and Qatar Airways.

LaGuardia is in East Elmhurst, on a narrow strip between the Grand Central Parkway and Flushing Bay, and it is the closest of the three to Midtown. Scheduled flights use two terminals. Terminal B serves Air Canada, American, Frontier, JetBlue, Porter, Southwest and United, and Terminal C is run by Delta. Terminal A, the original Marine Air Terminal at the western end of the field, now serves private aviation through Modern Aviation.

Newark Liberty is in New Jersey, nine miles west-southwest of Manhattan, and the road runs through the Lincoln or Holland Tunnel and down the New Jersey Turnpike. Terminal A, opened to passengers in January 2023, serves Air Canada, American, Delta, JetBlue and United. Terminal B carries more than 25 domestic and international airlines, among them Alaska, Lufthansa and SAS. Terminal C is run by United.

Teterboro is a general aviation field in Bergen County, New Jersey, about 12 miles north-northwest of Midtown, used by private and corporate aircraft and reached from Manhattan by the Lincoln Tunnel and Route 3.

Which airport a flight uses

LaGuardia's perimeter rule bars most nonstop flights to or from cities more than 1,500 miles away, with Saturdays and Denver the standing exceptions. Transatlantic and West Coast flights therefore leave from JFK or Newark, while LaGuardia handles the shorter runs to places such as Chicago, Atlanta, Florida and Toronto.

For timing, the terminal matters more than the airport. The JFK terminals are far enough apart on the loop that a car sent to Terminal 8 is not a short walk from Terminal 4, and airline assignments will change as the new terminals open. The flight number settles both the airport and the terminal, so it is the one detail to give when booking.

What changes at JFK in 2026 and 2027

JFK is partway through a Port Authority rebuild, and the airport lists the construction impacts as ongoing throughout 2026. Two new terminals are going up. New Terminal One, on the site of the demolished Terminal 2 next to Terminal 4, is now due to open its first phase in early 2027 with 13 gates plus one temporary gate, after missing its 2026 target, and the current Terminal 1 is to be demolished once it opens. A new Terminal 6, built for JetBlue and partner airlines on the site of the old Terminals 6 and 7, will connect to Terminal 5 and open in phases. Its first gates are due in late 2026 and the full terminal in 2028, with Terminal 7 demolished after the first phase.

Three things follow for anyone arriving or leaving by car. The airport warns of detours and closures on parts of its road network and of congestion at the terminal frontages. Since May 2025, ride app and car service pickups for Terminals 5 and 7 have been moved to a designated lot at the Howard Beach AirTrain station, a free AirTrain ride from the terminals, while drop-offs still happen in front of the terminal. And opening dates at JFK have already slipped, so a flight booked for early 2027 is worth checking against the airline's terminal notice a few days before departure.

Curb rules that set pickup timing

No car can wait at the arrivals door at any of the three airports. JFK curbs are for active pickups and drop-offs only, and unattended vehicles are towed. LaGuardia and Newark state the rule outright: a driver may not wait at the curb unless the passenger and bags are already there, and otherwise waits in the cell phone lot until called.

So an airport pickup runs in two stages. The chauffeur holds nearby while the flight lands, then comes to the curb or the designated pickup area once you have your bags, confirming the exact spot by phone or WhatsApp. How that exchange works, and which lot the car waits in at each airport, is set out in meeting your driver at JFK, LaGuardia and Newark.

The flight is tracked, so the pickup moves with the aircraft. Domestic arrivals include 45 minutes of complimentary waiting and international arrivals 60, counted from touchdown, and every other pickup carries a 15 minute grace period. If you would rather not coordinate at all, meet and greet puts the chauffeur inside the terminal with a name sign, as an add-on plus any airport parking charge. Pickup detail for each building is under JFK terminal pickups and LaGuardia arrivals.

How early to leave

TSA does not publish a single arrival time. It tells travelers to check with the airline, because times vary by airport and date, and to allow for parking or shuttles, check-in, a boarding pass and security screening. The MyTSA app shows how busy a checkpoint usually is at a given hour, based on historical data. Airlines set their own check-in and bag drop cutoffs, and that figure is the one to plan backward from.

Then add the road, which varies more than the distance suggests. For JFK the slow point is often the Kew Gardens interchange in central Queens, where the Van Wyck meets the Grand Central and Jackie Robinson parkways and nearly 600,000 vehicles pass each day, several miles before the terminals. Every LaGuardia trip comes off the Grand Central Parkway, so its ramps queue at peak. For Newark, every trip from Manhattan runs through the Lincoln or Holland Tunnel, and the tunnel approach sets the pace. Departure routes from each part of the island are set out under Manhattan chauffeur and black car service.

Bring the right ID. TSA has enforced REAL ID for domestic flights since May 7, 2025. A passport is accepted in its place, and as of September 2026 a traveler without acceptable ID pays a $45 TSA ConfirmID fee, and TSA does not guarantee it can verify the traveler's identity.

Rail links, and when a car is simpler

AirTrain JFK links every terminal to the subway and the Long Island Rail Road at Jamaica and to the subway at Howard Beach. It is free between terminals, and as of September 2026 the fare for entering or leaving at Jamaica or Howard Beach is $8.75, paid with a contactless card, a phone or an OMNY card. Trains run every 5 to 15 minutes.

LaGuardia has no rail link. The Q70 LaGuardia Link bus is free and runs to Terminals B and C from the Jackson Heights-Roosevelt Av and 74 St-Broadway subway stations.

AirTrain Newark is free between the terminals, parking and rental cars, and costs $8.75 to reach the Newark Liberty International Airport station on NJ Transit and Amtrak unless you already hold a rail ticket. It does not connect directly to Terminal A, which is a 15 minute covered walk or a 5 minute shuttle ride away, and the system is being modernized, with shuttle buses running whenever the train is out of service.

Rail makes sense when you travel light and the hotel sits on a subway line. A car is simpler with more than one large bag each, with children, with a stop on the way, or on a late arrival. The Business SUV class seats five with three large and three carry-on bags, the twelve seat Sprinter takes ten large bags with two carry-ons, and child seats are included at no charge. The flat airport rates apply in both directions.

Arriving from abroad

International arrivals take longer from touchdown to the door because of passport control, baggage and customs. The Mobile Passport Control app from U.S. Customs and Border Protection is free and accepted at both JFK and Newark, for U.S. citizens, lawful permanent residents, Canadian citizen visitors and returning Visa Waiver Program travelers with an approved ESTA. The 60 minutes of complimentary waiting on an international arrival starts at touchdown, and once it runs out, waiting is charged at a rate that varies by vehicle type.

When hourly service is the better option

A flat transfer works best when the traveler is going straight to one address. If the day after landing includes a hotel stop, a meeting, lunch and another transfer, hourly chauffeur service by vehicle class keeps one car and one chauffeur with you, with a three hour minimum. Instead of rebooking each leg, the vehicle and the luggage stay with you while the schedule shifts.

Key points

  • JFK curbs are for active pickups only, and since May 2025 car pickups at Terminals 5 and 7 use a lot at the Howard Beach AirTrain station
  • New Terminal One at JFK is due to open its first gates in early 2027
  • LaGuardia has no rail link, but the free Q70 bus runs to Terminals B and C
  • Domestic arrivals include 45 minutes of complimentary waiting and international arrivals 60, counted from touchdown
