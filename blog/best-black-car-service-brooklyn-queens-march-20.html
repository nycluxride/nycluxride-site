Brooklyn and Queens to JFK or LaGuardia: which airport, which road

March 20, 2026, updated September 28, 2026

From most Brooklyn and Queens addresses the airport run comes down to four roads, the BQE, the Belt Parkway, the Van Wyck and the Grand Central Parkway, plus one narrow stretch below Brooklyn Heights.

Which airport is nearer from where

Both of the city's commercial airports are in Queens. JFK sits in Jamaica on the edge of Jamaica Bay and LaGuardia in East Elmhurst on Flushing Bay, around ten miles apart, so the nearer one depends on where the trip starts. From Astoria, Long Island City, Sunnyside or Jackson Heights it is LaGuardia by a wide margin. From Jamaica, Ozone Park, Howard Beach or the Rockaways it is JFK. Forest Hills, Flushing and Bayside sit between the two, and there the traffic of the day usually decides. The split is set out area by area, with the flat rate to each airport, under Queens pickups by neighborhood.

JFK is the nearer field for most Brooklyn addresses. South and central Brooklyn run east along the shore on the Belt Parkway, or out Atlantic Avenue to Conduit Boulevard. From Greenpoint and Williamsburg, LaGuardia is a short run over the Kosciuszko Bridge. LaGuardia carries the lower flat rate from anywhere in the city, but from Bay Ridge or Sheepshead Bay it is often the longer drive, because the direct road north is the BQE, and its slow section runs through Downtown Brooklyn and Williamsburg.

The airline can settle the question before geography does. Under a Port Authority rule in place since 1984, LaGuardia flights may not serve points farther than 1,500 miles away, except on Saturdays and to Denver, so a nonstop to the West Coast or Europe leaves from JFK or Newark. At LaGuardia the scheduled airlines use Terminals B and C, and Terminal C is Delta's. How arrivals are met at each field is set out under JFK terminal pickups and LaGuardia terminal pickups.

The BQE and its narrow middle

The Brooklyn-Queens Expressway, Interstate 278, is the road that joins the two boroughs. It picks up from the Gowanus Expressway at the Hugh L. Carey Tunnel exit, runs beneath the Brooklyn Heights Promenade, crosses Newtown Creek into Queens on the Kosciuszko Bridge, and near its northern end splits into two branches that merge into the Grand Central Parkway. It is the standard LaGuardia route from Brooklyn.

The stretch to plan around lies between Atlantic Avenue and the Brooklyn Bridge, where the road rides the triple cantilever below the Promenade. In August 2021 the city cut that section from three lanes to two in each direction, a change officials said could extend the structure's life by 20 years, and in August 2023 it began ticketing overweight vehicles there. About 130,000 vehicles a day use the 1.5 mile corridor, more than twice what it was designed to carry. In August 2026 the city announced a $4 billion, ten year repair plan that keeps two lanes each way, with construction expected to begin around 2030, so the narrow section will shape Brooklyn airport runs for years.

A pickup in Carroll Gardens, Cobble Hill or Red Hook bound for LaGuardia meets that section at the start of the trip, and the corridor slows badly through the afternoon, so an afternoon departure from those blocks needs more margin than the mileage suggests. From Williamsburg or Greenpoint the run to LaGuardia joins the BQE beyond it and never touches the cantilever.

Over Newtown Creek on the Kosciuszko Bridge

The Kosciuszko Bridge carries the BQE over Newtown Creek from Greenpoint to Maspeth. The 1939 truss bridge that stood there has been replaced by a pair of cable-stayed spans. The Queens-bound span opened in April 2017 and the Brooklyn-bound span in August 2019, with nine traffic lanes between them and a bike and pedestrian path on the Brooklyn-bound side. For Greenpoint and Williamsburg it is the direct line to LaGuardia, the BQE north over the creek and then the Grand Central Parkway east into East Elmhurst.

The Belt, the Jackie Robinson and the Van Wyck

JFK from south and central Brooklyn is usually the Belt Parkway. It starts at the Gowanus Expressway in Bay Ridge and follows the shore east past Marine Park, Mill Basin and Canarsie to the airport exits. It is the shortest line on the map and the least predictable in practice, which is why a Bay Ridge pickup for an early flight is a distance problem as much as a traffic one.

The Belt, like other parkways in the state, is closed to commercial traffic, and NYC DOT says trucks and commercial vehicles cannot use most parkways in the city. With those restrictions and the parkways' height limits, a twelve seat Sprinter is routed to JFK on the expressways rather than along the shore, and its timing follows that route, not the sedan's.

The Jackie Robinson Parkway is the diagonal through the middle. It runs from the junction of Jamaica, Pennsylvania and Bushwick Avenues in East New York to the Kew Gardens Interchange, a direct line from Bushwick and Cypress Hills toward the Van Wyck. It was laid out to disturb as few graves as possible in the cemeteries it crosses, its curves held the original design speed to 35 miles per hour, and it is closed to commercial vehicles as well.

From northern Brooklyn and most of Queens, JFK is the Van Wyck Expressway, Interstate 678, which runs south through Queens to the airport. Its pinch point is the Kew Gardens Interchange, where the Van Wyck meets the Grand Central Parkway, the Jackie Robinson Parkway, Queens Boulevard and Union Turnpike, and which handles nearly 600,000 vehicles a day. New York State finished rebuilding the interchange in 2022, and on the way back from JFK it is still where most northbound delay forms, several miles short of the terminals.

Tolls on the way, and where they start

The main roads between Brooklyn or Queens and the two airports carry no tolls. None of the MTA's nine toll bridges and tunnels sits on the BQE, the Kosciuszko Bridge, the Belt Parkway, the Jackie Robinson, the Van Wyck or the Grand Central Parkway east of the Robert F. Kennedy Bridge, so a Park Slope to JFK or an Astoria to LaGuardia run adds nothing for tolls.

Tolls start at the edges. From Rockaway Beach the run north on Cross Bay Boulevard to JFK crosses the Cross Bay Veterans Memorial Bridge, an MTA toll bridge, as is the Marine Parkway-Gil Hodges Memorial Bridge between the peninsula and Brooklyn. A Bronx leg adds the Bronx-Whitestone, Throgs Neck or Robert F. Kennedy Bridge, and a Manhattan leg through the Hugh L. Carey or Queens-Midtown Tunnel adds another.

Newark is where tolls add up. From Brooklyn the usual route runs over the Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge, along the Staten Island Expressway and across the Goethals Bridge. The Verrazzano collects tolls in both directions. The Goethals and the Holland Tunnel collect only from traffic heading into New York, while the Hugh L. Carey Tunnel charges both ways, so either route costs more in tolls on the return from Newark than on the way out. Flat airport rates from NYC LUX RIDE exclude tolls, so the route decides that line of the invoice.

When a train is the simpler answer

For one traveler with a carry-on, rail is worth a look. The Long Island Rail Road runs a shuttle between Atlantic Terminal in Brooklyn and Jamaica, where AirTrain JFK connects to the terminals, and the A train reaches the Howard Beach AirTrain station. AirTrain costs $8.75, charged on entering or leaving at Jamaica or Howard Beach, and rides between terminals are free. LaGuardia has no rail link, but the Q70 LaGuardia Link is a free bus to Terminals B and C from the Jackson Heights-Roosevelt Avenue and 74th Street-Broadway subway hub.

The car makes more sense with luggage, children or a group leaving one address. The Business SUV class takes five passengers with three large cases and three carry-ons, and the twelve seat Sprinter carries ten large bags and two carry-ons, door to terminal with no change at Jamaica. Child seats in every size come at no charge. Vehicles and hourly work for the borough are set out under black car service in Brooklyn, and Brooklyn pickups by neighborhood covers the local streets and the airport routes from each part of the borough.

Key points

  • JFK is nearer for most of Brooklyn and for southern Queens, LaGuardia for western and northern Queens
  • The BQE between Atlantic Avenue and the Brooklyn Bridge has carried two lanes each way since August 2021, and the city's repair plan puts construction around 2030
  • The BQE, Belt, Van Wyck and Grand Central routes to both airports carry no tolls, while the Rockaway bridges are tolled and a Newark run pays the Verrazzano
