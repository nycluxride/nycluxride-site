Planning wedding and event transportation in NYC

March 27, 2026, updated October 12, 2026, 6 min read

Planning wedding transportation in NYC starts with a few fixed times, joined by short drives that often take longer than they should. Below is a timeline built backward from the ceremony, with City Hall rules, curb limits and the dates that need extra time.

In short

  • Plan transport backward from the ceremony, the photographer's window and the reception doors
  • A car may stop at a No Standing sign but can't wait there, and each address needs a pickup corner
  • A full wedding day can run 6 to 10 hours, billed as one hourly booking for each car

Start with the times that can't change

Every wedding day has three or four times that can't change. They are when the ceremony starts, when the photographer has to finish, when the reception doors open and when the venue closes. Plan the cars backward from those times. With a 4:00 PM ceremony, start with when the wedding party has to leave the getting-ready address to be seated before the guests. Then add the Midtown traffic on the way.

Count people and bags at the same time. A group of 10 with garment bags, a florist's box and a change of shoes needs more room than 10 people with nothing in their hands. A Luxury Sprinter seats 12 and takes 10 large and 2 small bags. A Business SUV takes 5 passengers with 3 large and 3 small bags, and a First Class sedan takes 3 with 2 large and 1 small. Each car's seats, bags and hourly rate are in our fleet by class.

A wedding day timeline

This plan assumes a 4:00 PM ceremony in Manhattan, portraits in a park afterward and a 6:00 PM reception at a second venue. It uses one Sprinter for the wedding party and a First Class sedan for the couple. For a different ceremony time, move each line by the same amount.

  1. 1:30 PM, getting ready. The Sprinter arrives at the hotel or apartment where the wedding party is dressing. Take the photos of everyone boarding here, where there's time.
  2. 2:45 PM, the couple. If the couple get ready in different places, the sedan goes to the second address and waits at a legal spot nearby.
  3. 3:15 PM, the wedding party leaves. We plan a 20-minute drive as 40. The party is inside by 3:40 PM, and the Sprinter has left the entrance before the guests arrive.
  4. 3:50 PM, the couple arrives. The sedan waits around the corner and pulls up to the entrance only for the drop-off.
  5. 4:30 PM, the ceremony ends. Both cars pick up at a corner you've agreed on.
  6. 4:45 PM to 5:45 PM, portraits. An hour in the park with the photographer, and the Sprinter comes back to the same gate at the end.
  7. 6:00 PM, arrival at the reception. The party gets out, and the Sprinter waits nearby for the evening, or drives guests between the reception and a hotel.
  8. 11:00 PM, last rides. The couple's car and the last guest trip leave from the reception. The ride home is part of the same booking, and the car stays with you until the end.

From 1:30 PM to about 11:30 PM is 10 hours, the long end for a full wedding day. The First Class sedan for the couple, from 2:45 PM, is a second booking of about 9 hours. Each car is billed for its whole booking, with a 3-hour minimum, before NYC sales tax and the Black Car Fund. Tolls and parking are charged after the ride, and the gratuity is optional. Current hourly rates by class are on the rates page.

City Hall ceremonies

The Office of the City Clerk runs the Manhattan Marriage Bureau at 141 Worth Street, at the corner of Centre Street. It is open by appointment only. Ceremonies and marriage licenses are booked through Project Cupid, walk-ins are not accepted, and the Clerk asks visitors not to arrive without a confirmation email.

New York law sets the order of the two visits. You can't marry within 24 hours of getting the license, and the license expires after 60 days. So the license and the ceremony are on different days. You can also get the license at a virtual appointment. Then the ceremony is the only visit in person.

On the day, plan around the appointment time. A 3-hour booking usually covers the drive downtown, the wait at the Clerk's office and a photo stop or lunch afterward. Send us the appointment time, the pickup address and the lunch reservation, and we'll plan the route from those.

Where a car can wait at a venue

A car can't wait at the door. NYC DOT rules allow a car to stop at a No Standing sign to drop off or pick up passengers, but not to wait there. A No Stopping sign allows neither. Double parking is never allowed, and city law limits idling to 3 minutes.

So for every address, the plan names two corners. One is where the party gets out. The other is where the car waits when the ceremony or portraits end. Your chauffeur waits at a legal spot nearby until you call or message. That takes a few minutes. Build them into the timeline.

Parks need a permit. Central Park's Conservatory Garden, through the Vanderbilt Gate off Fifth Avenue between 104th and 105th Streets, allows weddings by permit only. Each wedding permit gives you 90 minutes in the garden for the ceremony and photos. A professional or staged photo session needs a separate photography permit, and applications close 10 days before the date. The garden stays open to the public during your event. Plan a 90-minute stop and have the car back at the gate when it ends.

Dates that change the plan

The New York City Marathon is on the first Sunday of November. In 2026 that's November 1. It starts on Staten Island at the Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge, closed to traffic for the race, and goes through all five boroughs to a finish in Central Park. If your wedding is that weekend, we plan routes around the course.

NYC DOT also names Gridlock Alert Days, the weekdays it expects to be the slowest of the year. The rest of the 2026 list is November 18, 19 and 24, and 12 weekdays in December from the 2nd to the 23rd. For a Friday rehearsal dinner or a weekday ceremony on one of those dates, allow extra time for every trip.

Book early too. We have 2 Luxury Sprinters. If you need both, book them as soon as you book the venue.

One Sprinter, several cars or a guest shuttle

Separate cars work for the couple, the parents and anyone arriving alone. A First Class sedan or a Sedan & Electric car carries 3. A Business SUV carries 5 with room for luggage, good for a family coming from the airport straight to the rehearsal.

For a wedding party of 6 to 12, one Sprinter works better than several cars, because everyone arrives together. The Limo Sprinter has quilted bench seating with a bar and a screen, and the Jet Sprinter has two rows of captain's chairs facing a screen. See Sprinter limousine service for weddings and groups for both cabins and the event terms.

For a guest shuttle, our 2 Sprinters carry up to 24 guests per trip between a hotel and the venue. For 80 guests, that's several trips, each with the drive both ways. It works best when the hotel and the venue are close together.

Guests flying in the day before can use our flat airport rates from JFK, LaGuardia, Newark and Teterboro. We meet them at the curb or the car service pickup area and call or message them. Meet and greet inside the terminal, with a name sign, is an extra charge, plus any airport parking.
