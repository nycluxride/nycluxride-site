Planning wedding and event transportation in NYC

March 27, 2026, updated September 28, 2026

A New York wedding day runs on a few fixed times joined by short drives that rarely behave like short drives. The timeline below is built backward from the ceremony, with the City Hall rules, curb limits and calendar dates that decide how much buffer each leg needs.

Start from the times that cannot move

Every wedding day has three or four times that do not move: the ceremony start, the end of the photographer's window, the reception doors and the hour the venue closes. Transport is planned backward from those, not forward from the first pickup. With a four o'clock ceremony, the question is not when the car arrives in the morning. It is when the wedding party has to leave the getting ready address to be seated before the guests, and how much Midtown traffic sits in between.

Count the party and the bags at the same stage. Ten people with garment bags, a florist's box and a change of shoes for the reception is a different booking from ten people with nothing in hand. Each Luxury Sprinter seats 12 and takes 10 large plus 2 small bags. A Business SUV takes 5 passengers with 3 large and 3 small bags, and a First Class sedan takes 3 with 2 large and 1 small. Every vehicle is listed with its seats, bags and hourly rate in the fleet by class.

A wedding day timeline template

The template assumes a 4:00 p.m. ceremony in Manhattan, portraits in a park afterward and a 6:00 p.m. reception at a second venue, with one Sprinter for the wedding party and a First Class sedan for the couple. For a different ceremony time, move every line by the same amount.

  1. 1:30 p.m., getting ready. The Sprinter arrives at the hotel or apartment where the wedding party is dressing. Photographs of the party boarding are taken here, where there is time, rather than at the ceremony curb.
  2. 2:45 p.m., the couple. The sedan reaches the second getting ready address if the couple dress apart, and holds at a legal spot nearby rather than at the door.
  3. 3:15 p.m., wedding party departs. A drive of twenty minutes on the map is planned as forty, so the party is inside by 3:40 and the Sprinter has cleared the entrance before the guests arrive.
  4. 3:50 p.m., couple arrives. The sedan has been waiting around the corner and pulls up at the entrance only for the drop-off.
  5. 4:30 p.m., ceremony ends. Both vehicles collect at a named corner, not the venue door, because the car may pick up at the curb but may not wait there.
  6. 4:45 to 5:45 p.m., portraits. The photographer's hour in the park, with the Sprinter booked back at the same gate at the end of it.
  7. 6:00 p.m., reception arrival. The party unloads and the Sprinter holds nearby for the evening, or runs guests between the reception and a hotel.
  8. 11:00 p.m., late departures. The couple's car and the last guest run leave from the reception. The return sits on the same reservation, because a separate return at eleven at night is the hardest leg of the day to guarantee.

From 1:30 p.m. to about 11:30 p.m. is ten hours, the long end of the six to ten hours most full wedding days run. A First Class sedan held for the couple from 2:45 p.m. is a second block of about nine hours. Each vehicle is billed for its whole block, with three hours the minimum. Taxes, tolls and a standard gratuity are added to the final invoice, and the total is confirmed at booking. Current hourly rates by class are on the rates page.

City Hall ceremonies

The Office of the City Clerk runs the Manhattan Marriage Bureau at 141 Worth Street, at the corner of Centre Street. It is open by appointment only. Ceremonies and marriage licenses are booked through Project Cupid, walk-ins are not accepted, and the Clerk asks visitors not to arrive without a confirmation email.

New York law sets the order of the two visits. A marriage cannot be solemnized within 24 hours of the license being issued, and the license lapses after 60 days, so the license and the ceremony fall on different days. The license can also be issued at a virtual appointment, which leaves the ceremony as the only visit in person.

On the ceremony day the appointment time is the fixed point. A three hour booking usually covers the drive downtown, the wait at the Clerk's office and a photo stop or lunch afterward, the shortest version of wedding transport there is. Give dispatch the appointment time, the pickup address and the lunch reservation, and the route is planned from all three.

Where a car can wait at a venue

The most common gap in a wedding plan is the assumption that the car waits at the door. NYC DOT rules allow a car to stop at a No Standing sign to drop off or pick up passengers, but not to wait there, and a No Stopping sign allows neither. Double parking a passenger vehicle is illegal at all times, whatever the purpose or duration, and city law makes idling for longer than three minutes illegal.

So the plan names two corners for every address: the one where the party is dropped and the one where the car is waiting when the ceremony or the portraits end. The chauffeur holds at a legal spot nearby and is called in by phone or WhatsApp. That takes a few minutes, and those minutes belong inside the timeline rather than on top of it.

Parks add a permit. Central Park's Conservatory Garden, entered through the Vanderbilt Gate off Fifth Avenue between 104th and 105th Streets, takes weddings by permit only. Each wedding permit includes 90 minutes in the chosen garden for the ceremony and photographs, a professional or staged photo session needs a photography permit, and applications close 10 days before the date. The garden stays open to the public during a permitted event, so the 90 minutes is the length of the stop and the car is booked back at the gate when it ends.

Dates that change the plan

The New York City Marathon runs on the first Sunday of November, which is November 1 in 2026. It starts on Staten Island at the Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge, which is closed to traffic for the race, and runs through all five boroughs to a finish in Central Park. A wedding that weekend needs routes planned around the course rather than across it.

NYC DOT also publishes Gridlock Alert Days, the weekdays it expects to be the slowest of the year. The 2026 list closes with November 18, 19 and 24, and twelve weekdays in December from the 2nd to the 23rd. A Friday rehearsal dinner or a weekday ceremony on one of those dates needs extra buffer on every leg.

Lead time matters as much as traffic. Booking at least 24 hours ahead is the minimum recommendation, and a Saturday needs more. The fleet has two Luxury Sprinters, so a date that needs both is worth reserving as soon as the venue is.

One Sprinter, several cars or a guest shuttle

Private cars suit the couple, the parents and anyone who needs to arrive on their own. A First Class sedan or a Sedan & Electric car carries three. A Business SUV carries five with room for luggage, which suits a family arriving from the airport straight to the rehearsal.

A wedding party of six to twelve is where one Sprinter beats several cars, because it arrives as one group at one time. The Limo Sprinter has quilted bench seating with a bar and a screen, and the Jet Sprinter has two rows of captain's chairs facing a screen. Both cabins and the event terms are set out under Sprinter limousine service for weddings and groups.

A guest shuttle is a different job. Two Sprinters move up to 24 guests per run between a hotel and the venue, so a guest list of eighty needs several loops, and every loop adds the drive both ways. It works best when the hotel and the venue are close together.

Guests flying in the day before ride on the flat airport rates from JFK, Newark, Teterboro and LaGuardia. The standard pickup is at the curb or the designated pickup area, with the chauffeur coordinating by phone or WhatsApp. Meet and greet inside the terminal, with a name sign, is an add-on plus any airport parking charge.

Key points

  • Plan transport backward from the ceremony, the photographer's window and the reception doors
  • A car may drop off at a No Standing sign but may not wait there, so name a pickup corner for every address
  • Most full wedding days run six to ten hours, billed as one hourly block for each vehicle
