Meeting your driver at JFK, LaGuardia and Newark

April 24, 2026, updated September 28, 2026

None of New York's three airports lets a car wait at the arrivals door, so every pickup comes down to a short exchange of messages. Where the chauffeur waits, what to send and when, and how to tell your car from an offer you did not book.

Why the car cannot wait at the door

The arrivals curb at each airport is for active pickups only. JFK keeps its terminal curbsides for pickups and drop-offs in progress, and a vehicle left unattended is towed. LaGuardia and Newark add the detail that shapes every pickup: a driver may not wait at the curb unless the passenger and the bags are already there, and if the passenger is not ready, the driver waits in the cell phone lot until called.

That rule decides when the car moves. The chauffeur tracks the flight and knows when it lands, but landing is not the moment to come to the door. The moment is when you have your bags and are walking out, and the only way the chauffeur learns that is a message from you. A phone call works too, but a WhatsApp thread keeps the flight details, the terminal, the door and a live map in one place, and it runs over any data or Wi-Fi connection, which suits a traveler arriving on a foreign phone plan.

Where drivers wait for your message

JFK has two waiting lots. The Cell Phone Lot near the JFK Expressway is the closest to Terminals 5, 7 and 8 and less than five minutes from every terminal. The free wait lot at the Lefferts Boulevard AirTrain station, reached from the Van Wyck Expressway or the Belt Parkway near the airport entrance, is the closest to Terminals 1 and 4, and drivers there must stay in their vehicles.

LaGuardia's cell phone lot is off the airport on 94th Street between 23rd Avenue and Ditmars Boulevard, less than 10 minutes from all terminals, and as of September 2026 it is open every day from 7:00 a.m. to 11:59 p.m. Newark's lot sits near the airport entrance beside the P4 parking garage, with more than 100 spaces, less than five minutes from every terminal.

Those lots are why the car may not be in sight when you land. The chauffeur is parked a few minutes away and waiting for the message that says you are ready.

Where you are met

The standard pickup is at the curb or at the designated pickup area for your terminal, and the chauffeur confirms the exact spot by phone or WhatsApp once you are on the arrivals level with your bags. At JFK, construction has moved some pickups away from the terminals. Since May 2025, car service pickups for Terminals 5 and 7 have used a designated lot at the Howard Beach AirTrain station, a free AirTrain ride from the terminal. At LaGuardia the chauffeur meets you on the arrivals level of Terminal B or C and names the door. Each building is covered in turn under JFK terminal pickups and LaGuardia arrivals.

Do not follow the Ride App Pick Up signs by default. They lead to the Uber and Lyft zones, such as level 2 of the Terminal B garage at LaGuardia and floor 3 of the Terminal C garage at Newark, and the place to be is the one the chauffeur names in the message.

If you would rather skip the coordination, add meet and greet. The chauffeur waits inside the terminal with a name sign and takes the bags from there, as an add-on plus any airport parking charge, listed with the other airport transfer terms. It is the better choice with a lot of luggage, with children, or on a first visit.

Four messages worth sending

A quick pickup needs few messages, each sent at the right moment.

  1. Before you fly: the airline and flight number, the number of passengers and bags, and whether you want the curb or meet and greet. If someone else is traveling, the name on the booking should be the person the chauffeur is meeting.
  2. On landing: that you are on the ground and which terminal, for example "Landed, Terminal 4, heading to baggage claim." Say so if the bags are slow or the passport line is long.
  3. With bags in hand: the door number or the pickup sign you are standing at. This is the message that moves the car, so send it once you are ready to walk out.
  4. If you cannot see each other: your live location. In WhatsApp, open the chat, tap the plus button, then Location, then Share live location, and choose how long to share it.

Live location is end-to-end encrypted, so only the people in the chat can see it, and it stops when the time you chose runs out or whenever you tap Stop sharing. On an iPhone, set the WhatsApp location permission to Always first, under Settings, Privacy and Security, Location Services.

Send these to the NYC LUX RIDE WhatsApp line, or use phone, email or the inquiry form if WhatsApp is not an option.

When the flight changes

A delay needs no message. The flight is watched in real time and the pickup moves to the new arrival time at no extra cost. Domestic arrivals include 45 minutes of complimentary waiting and international arrivals 60, counted from touchdown, and every other pickup carries a 15 minute grace period. An early landing is the case worth a message, because the car comes forward as soon as the chauffeur knows you are out. If the plan after landing grows into a hotel stop and a meeting, hourly chauffeur service keeps the same car and chauffeur for the day.

International arrivals

Arrivals from abroad take longer from touchdown to the door because of passport control, baggage and customs, and the complimentary wait on an international arrival is 60 minutes rather than 45. The Mobile Passport Control app from U.S. Customs and Border Protection is free and accepted at JFK and Newark for U.S. citizens, lawful permanent residents, Canadian citizen visitors and returning Visa Waiver Program travelers with an approved ESTA.

Send the landed message as soon as the phone connects, then a second one when you clear customs. The chauffeur can see the landing time but not the immigration hall, so the second message is the one that counts.

If someone offers you a ride

Licensed drivers in New York City are not allowed to solicit riders. The Taxi and Limousine Commission says TLC drivers are prohibited from asking people if they need a ride, and it lists airports first among the places where illegal ride activity can be common. Those rides are uninsured and frequently overcharged.

A booked chauffeur already knows your name and flight, and you already have the chauffeur's messages, so anyone else offering a car in the arrivals hall is not your ride. Taxis come from the official taxi line, every TLC vehicle has T&LC embossed at the bottom of its license plate, and violations can be reported to 311.

Key points

  • At LaGuardia and Newark a driver may not wait at the curb unless the passenger and bags are already there
  • Each airport has a cell phone lot within 10 minutes of the terminals where drivers wait for your call
  • The message that brings the car is the one sent with bags in hand, naming the door
  • Meet and greet inside the terminal is an add-on, plus any airport parking charge
