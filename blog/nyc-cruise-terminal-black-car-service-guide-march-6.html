Car service to the Manhattan and Brooklyn cruise terminals

March 6, 2026, updated September 28, 2026

Where the Manhattan and Brooklyn cruise terminals are, how cars reach each pier, and how to size the vehicle for embarkation and return days.

Which terminal your ship uses

New York City has two cruise terminals, and a third port sits across the harbor in New Jersey. They are far enough apart that the wrong one can cost most of a morning, so the first thing to check is the terminal printed in the cruise line's documents rather than the city on the itinerary. Lines move ships between the three from one season to the next.

The Manhattan Cruise Terminal is at 711 Twelfth Avenue in Hell's Kitchen, on the Hudson River between West 46th and West 54th Streets. Ships berth at Piers 88 and 90, and the terminal building stands at the end of West 48th Street, a short run from any Manhattan pickup in Midtown or on the West Side.

The Brooklyn Cruise Terminal is at Pier 12 in Red Hook, on Buttermilk Channel opposite Governors Island. The terminal's address is 210 Clinton Wharf, and every vehicle enters through the main gate where Bowne Street meets Imlay Street.

Cape Liberty Cruise Port, the third, stands on the site of the former Military Ocean Terminal in Bayonne, New Jersey, and its wayfinding address is 4 Port Terminal Boulevard. Royal Caribbean is one of its primary tenants. Cars reach it from Exit 14A of the New Jersey Turnpike, then Route 440 south for a mile and a half and a left into the Bayonne Ocean Terminal, with the cruise building two miles straight ahead.

The port's most recent published guidance, from 2025, sets strict curb rules. On sailing day, passengers and bags are dropped together at luggage Zones 1 to 5 in front of the terminal, and anyone arriving less than 90 minutes before departure is not allowed to sail. On return day, car services collect at Zones 6 to 10 along the main building, a cell phone waiting lot sits beside the terminal, and a car standing or parked in front of the terminal can be towed or fined. Check the cruise line's documents for the current zones before sailing day. Bayonne is outside New York City and outside the published rate list, so confirm a Cape Liberty trip with dispatch before planning around it.

Reaching Piers 88 and 90

Every car enters the Manhattan terminal from the north, at the corner of 55th Street and Twelfth Avenue, which is State Route 9A along this stretch. A car coming from the Lincoln Tunnel or from Lower Manhattan drives north on Twelfth Avenue past the piers and turns left at 55th Street. From Westchester, the George Washington Bridge or the Upper West Side, the Henry Hudson Parkway runs south, continues below 72nd Street as the West Side Highway and exits to the right at 55th Street. From Midtown the car takes 55th Street west and crosses Twelfth Avenue into the terminal.

One entrance for both piers is why the approach matters more than the distance. The terminal's own guidance is that most of its vehicle traffic is buses, shuttles, taxis and drop-offs, and every one of them makes the same turn at 55th Street on a sailing morning.

Drop-off is at street level of the pier where the ship is berthed, with signs on the pillars naming each vessel. Porters take checked bags there and move them to the ship, and passengers go up by elevator or escalator to the customs hall. Passports, boarding documents, medication and anything valuable belong in the carry-on, because the checked bags leave with the porters at the curb.

The terminal's directions name 59th Street-Columbus Circle on the A, B, C, D and 1, and the walk from there runs down Eighth Avenue and along 52nd Street to Twelfth Avenue. That is easy with a backpack and a long way with two large cases each, which is the part of the morning a car removes.

Reaching Red Hook

The Brooklyn terminal sits in Red Hook, Brooklyn, which has no subway station. The nearest stations are Carroll Street and Smith-9th Streets on the F and G, the B61 bus stops at Pioneer Street and Van Brunt Street about four long blocks from the entrance, and NYC Ferry's South Brooklyn route calls at the Red Hook landing. All three suit a passenger traveling light. With luggage, cruise day in this part of the borough is a car day, which is why Brooklyn Cruise Terminal pickups are worth booking ahead.

From Manhattan the route is the Hugh L. Carey Tunnel into Brooklyn, a short stretch of the westbound Brooklyn-Queens Expressway, and its first exit, Exit 26 for Hamilton Avenue. The service road runs to Van Brunt Street, and two blocks along Van Brunt a right turn onto Bowne Street reaches the gate. The tunnel is tolled, and on the way back it lands in the Financial District.

From JFK the Belt Parkway runs west across southern Brooklyn to the Gowanus Expressway, which reaches the same Hamilton Avenue exit. Because the parkways carry commercial vehicle and height restrictions, a Sprinter booking is routed on the expressways instead of the Belt. From New Jersey the terminal's directions use the Goethals Bridge, the Staten Island Expressway and the Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge, then the Gowanus north to Exit 26.

Embarkation morning: bags pick the vehicle

The terminals' published guidance is that ships usually dock between 7 and 8 a.m., baggage processing starts no earlier than 9:30 a.m., and boarding generally begins between 11:30 a.m. and 12:30 p.m. The latest check-in time belongs to the cruise line and is printed in the travel documents. Neither terminal stores luggage, so arriving long before bags are accepted means standing in the hall with them.

A cruise morning moves a lot of luggage against a fixed deadline, so bag capacity picks the vehicle before seat count does. A Business SUV takes up to 5 passengers with 3 large and 3 small bags. A First Class sedan takes 3 passengers with 2 large and 1 small. Two couples with two large cases each already need more than one SUV, and that is where the twelve seat Sprinter fits, with room for 10 large and 2 small bags. Passenger and bag capacity for all twelve vehicles is listed by class.

An hourly booking suits a morning with stops before the pier, such as a hotel checkout, a relative's apartment and a pharmacy. The car and the bags stay together between stops, and the pier is the last one. Every vehicle class books by the hour with a three hour minimum. See hourly rates by class.

Return day and customs

Disembarkation runs in reverse. Porters position the bags in the baggage hall, passengers collect them and pass the U.S. Customs and Border Protection inspection before leaving the building, and only then reach the taxi stand and the holding lot for prearranged cars outside. Mobile Passport Control, the CBP app that shortens the line at JFK and Newark, is offered at only four seaports as of September 2026, Miami, Palm Beach, Port Everglades and San Juan, so it does not help in New York or Bayonne.

The same terminal guidance says passengers who parked their own cars are often through customs and off the property by 10 to 10:30 a.m. A pier pickup carries the standard 15 minute grace period rather than the 45 or 60 minutes that apply to airport arrivals, and waiting charges then run from the original scheduled time. A car booked for the moment the ship docks is likely to spend that window waiting. A ready-to-exit message, sent once every bag is on the cart and naming the exit door, gives a more accurate pickup time than the ship's scheduled arrival.

From the pier, the airport run is one flat rate to JFK, Newark or LaGuardia, before taxes, tolls and gratuity. From Red Hook, JFK is usually the Belt Parkway and LaGuardia the Brooklyn-Queens Expressway north to the Grand Central Parkway. From Piers 88 and 90, Newark is the Lincoln Tunnel and the New Jersey Turnpike. For a late flight, an hourly booking keeps the bags in the car through lunch and a stop or two, with the airport as the last stop of the day.

Key points

  • Manhattan sailings use Piers 88 and 90 on Twelfth Avenue, and every car enters at 55th Street
  • Brooklyn sailings use Pier 12 in Red Hook, where cars enter at Bowne and Imlay Streets and no subway reaches the pier
  • Mobile Passport Control is not offered at the New York and New Jersey seaports, so returning passengers clear customs in the terminal hall
