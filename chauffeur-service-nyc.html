A chauffeur, not a dispatch queue

An hourly booking assigns one vehicle and one chauffeur to your reservation for its whole length. Between the first pickup and the last drop the car is not returned to a pool and replaced by whoever happens to be nearest. The same chauffeur learns the order of the stops, keeps your bags in the trunk while you are inside, and is at the curb when you come back out.

That is the working difference between a chauffeur and a ride. A ride ends at the destination. Chauffeured work is held open for a block of time, which is why it is billed by the hour and carries a three hour minimum rather than a per trip fare.

Standing time is part of what you reserved. If a meeting runs forty minutes long, nothing is re-dispatched and no second fare starts. The hours are already committed and the car is already outside. When hourly beats separate rides is worked through in hourly or point to point in Manhattan.

Discretion is a working practice, not a promise

Discretion is easy to claim and mostly consists of small operational habits. The vehicles carry no livery and no roof sign, so a car standing outside a building tells a passer-by nothing about who is inside it or why.

A chauffeur who does not open a conversation is not being cold. A phone call taken in the back seat is treated as a call the chauffeur cannot hear, and a colleague or a competitor named during it goes no further than the cabin.

The remaining items are unglamorous and matter more than any of it. Cabin temperature set before you get in. Audio off unless somebody asks for it. Doors opened on the sidewalk side wherever the block allows. Luggage loaded and lifted out without needing to be asked twice.

Route knowledge is the last piece. A chauffeur who has driven the same district for years knows which service entrance a tower actually unlocks at eight in the morning and which one stays shut until nine, and that knowledge shortens a day more reliably than speed does.

Billed by the hour, by vehicle class

Hourly and as directed work is billed by vehicle class with a three hour minimum. See hourly rates by class.

A straight run to a terminal does not need to be booked hourly, because airport transfers are flat rated from any New York City address. Bag capacity by vehicle decides the class, and the wider service list is under services.

Airport legs inside an hourly day

A terminal drop at the end of an hourly day is a decision rather than an assumption, because there are two ways to write it and they do not cost the same.

If the drop is the last stop of a day already on the clock, it sits inside the hours you reserved and nothing further applies. If the airport run is the entire job, the flat figure is almost always the cheaper of the two, and a flat figure is indifferent to what the parkway is doing on the way out.

Inbound legs work in reverse. Flights are monitored in real time and the pickup slides with the schedule when an arrival slips, so an inbound leg is better written as a flat transfer with the flight number attached than as the opening hour of an hourly day. At John F. Kennedy International Airport the chauffeur meets riders at the curb or the designated pickup area and confirms the spot by phone or WhatsApp once the bags are collected.

Meet and greet inside near baggage claim, with a name sign, is an add-on plus any airport parking charge. The complimentary window is 45 minutes on a domestic arrival, 60 on an international one, and 15 minutes on everything else.

Extending a booking once it has started

Days run long. An hourly reservation can usually be extended while it is still running, and the limit is not willingness but whatever assignment is already sitting behind yours on that vehicle.

Which makes flagging a likely overrun early the single most useful habit a rider has. A message to dispatch an hour out gives them room to hold the vehicle. A message ten minutes before the scheduled finish may not, because the same car can already be committed on the other side of town.

Shortening runs the other way and is simpler. The three hour minimum still applies, so a day that wraps up after two hours is billed as three rather than two.

Cancellation terms sit apart from all of this, because they turn on notice given rather than on hours used. They are stated at booking and confirmed in writing with the reservation.

Corporate accounts and repeat bookings

An assistant booking six rides a week should not be entering a card six times. Corporate accounts carry monthly billing, priority scheduling and dedicated support, and the month arrives as one invoice with the trips itemized on it.

New clients may be asked to complete a credit card authorization form before the first reservation is confirmed. It is a fraud control, it happens once at the start of the relationship, and it is not repeated per booking.

Notice matters more than volume. Twenty four hours ahead secures a specific vehicle class rather than whatever is unassigned, and same day requests are accepted when the fleet allows.

Where the chauffeur works

Hourly days concentrate where the stops are close together and the curb is not. New York City rules allow a passenger pickup at a No Standing curb but no waiting there, double parking is illegal at all times, and idling is limited to three minutes, or one minute beside a school or park, so the car holds nearby and returns on a message. These four areas account for most as directed work, and the full list is under service areas.

  • Manhattan

    Midtown, Uptown and Downtown stops on one reservation rather than five.

  • Financial District

    Wall Street, Battery Park and Seaport pickups before an early flight.

  • Upper East Side

    Residential pickups and executive appointments along Museum Mile.

  • Long Island City

    Office and studio work across the river by the Queensboro Bridge.

Questions about chauffeured work

All nineteen questions

How do I book a chauffeur in NYC?

Book online at customer.moovs.app or call +1 (646) 775-0556 with the pickup address, the destination, the date and time and the passenger count. Dispatch replies in writing with the vehicle class and the fare, and handles later changes to airport timing, added stops and return legs.

How much does chauffeur service in NYC cost?

Every vehicle class books by the hour with a three hour minimum. Airport transfers are flat rated instead, from any New York City address. See all rates.

Can an hourly day end at the airport?

Yes. If the terminal drop is the last stop of a day already on the clock, it sits inside the hours reserved and nothing further applies. If the airport run is the whole job, the flat rate is almost always the cheaper of the two.

Is waiting time billed separately on an hourly booking?

No. Time spent standing between stops is inside the hours you reserved, so it is not a separate line. On airport pickups the free window is 45 minutes on domestic arrivals, 60 minutes on international arrivals and 15 minutes elsewhere, and after that waiting charges begin from the original scheduled pickup time and vary by vehicle class.

Does the same chauffeur stay with me all day?

On an hourly booking the vehicle and the chauffeur are assigned to your reservation for its full length rather than returned to dispatch between stops. Very long days can require a second chauffeur for hours of service reasons, and dispatch confirms that at booking rather than on the day.
