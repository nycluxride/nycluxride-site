Limousine service in New York City for weddings, prom and group travel

Two Luxury Sprinters that seat 12 and take 10 large plus 2 small bags, booked by the hour with a three hour minimum, plus sedans and SUVs for the smaller movements around the day.

What twelve seats actually change

A party split across four sedans arrives as four groups at four times, and somebody is always still in traffic when the photographer is ready. One vehicle that seats 12 removes the coordination problem instead of managing it.

The bag figure decides as often as the seat figure. Each Luxury Sprinter takes 10 large plus 2 small bags. A Business SUV takes 3 large plus 3 small for up to 5 passengers, and a First Class sedan takes 2 large plus 1 small for up to 3. Eight people with a case each do not fit in two sedans and do fit in one Sprinter.

Interior layout changes the hour as well. The Limo Sprinter is quilted bench seating with a bar and a screen, which suits a group celebrating between venues. The Jet Sprinter is two rows of captain's chairs facing a screen, which suits a group that wants to sit facing each other and talk.

Why the fleet is Sprinters rather than stretch sedans

NYC LUX RIDE runs high roof Sprinters and does not run stretch sedans, and the reason is practical rather than fashionable. A stretch is long, low and awkward to turn into a narrow driveway. A Sprinter is tall, shorter in the wheelbase for what it carries, and you stand upright in the cabin.

Standing height is the feature nobody asks about and everybody uses. Twelve people board through one sliding door in about a minute instead of shuffling along a bench, and a gown or a dinner jacket is not creased on the way in or out.

Height also changes photographs. A photographer can work inside the cabin without kneeling in the aisle, which is usually where the shots from the drive between ceremony and reception come from.

The exchange is luggage space, and it goes in the Sprinter's favor. A stretch sedan has very little of it. A Sprinter has a dedicated luggage bay behind the rear bench, and that bay is where the published figure of 10 large plus 2 small bags comes from.

Weddings

Ceremony, reception and the gap between them

Wedding transport is a movement plan more than a ride. The ceremony vehicle has to be in position before the guests are, and the reception vehicle has to be waiting while the photographs run long. Both of those are hours rather than trips, which is why event work is booked hourly.

A three hour minimum covers a single ceremony transfer. A full day of ceremony, photographs, reception and an after party is usually six to ten hours on one reservation, with the vehicle staged at each address rather than released between them. A timeline built backward from the ceremony is in planning wedding transportation in NYC.

  • StagingThe vehicle holds position between locations
  • LengthMost full days run six to ten hours
  • Luggage10 large plus 2 small bags per Sprinter

Prom and milestones

The questions a parent asks first

Prom is paid for by parents and ridden by teenagers, so the details that settle the booking are the ones a parent raises. A fixed pickup time. A professional chauffeur. One vehicle that holds the whole group instead of three cars leaving at three different times. School dates, New York alcohol law and an hour by hour night are in planning prom transportation in NYC.

The same shape covers birthdays, anniversaries and graduation dinners. A milestone evening has two immovable times, the reservation and the end of the night, and an hourly booking is what keeps the vehicle between them.

  • GroupUp to 12 passengers per Sprinter
  • BookingAt least 24 hours ahead is recommended

Evenings that are neither a wedding nor a prom

Most of the calendar is neither. Birthdays, anniversaries, graduation dinners, bachelor and bachelorette evenings, homecoming, a concert at an arena with nowhere to leave a car, a family reunion landing from three different airports on one afternoon.

They share a shape. Two immovable times, an unpredictable middle, and a group that would otherwise turn up in pieces. Reserving by the hour is what holds the vehicle across the unpredictable middle, and the three hour minimum covers the shortest version of any of them.

Infant, toddler and booster seats are available on request at no extra charge, which counts for more on the family versions of these evenings than on anything else.

The one thing worth deciding early is whether the return leg is part of the same reservation. A concert that finishes at eleven and a restaurant that finishes at ten are different problems, and a return leg booked separately at eleven at night is the hardest leg of the night to guarantee.

Planning the movement

Group bookings go wrong in the same five places every time. Working through these in order at the booking stage is what keeps the day from needing a second vehicle at short notice.

  1. Count the party and the bags separately. Twelve seats with twelve suitcases is a different reservation from twelve seats with hand luggage.
  2. Fix the immovable time first. A ceremony start or a flight departure sets the pickup, and the rest of the schedule is arranged backward from it.
  3. Decide whether the vehicle stays. If there is a gap of an hour or more at one address, an hourly booking that holds the car usually costs less than two one way bookings.
  4. List every address in order, including the ones that are only a photograph stop. The chauffeur plans the route from the full list, not from the first line of it.
  5. Confirm at least 24 hours ahead. That is what secures a named class rather than whatever is unassigned on the day.

Where event bookings usually run

Venues cluster, and so do the pickup problems around them. City Hall ceremonies run by appointment at the Manhattan Marriage Bureau, 141 Worth Street. Lincoln Center empties onto Columbus Avenue between 62nd and 66th Streets, so the car waits a block west on Amsterdam Avenue. Yankee Stadium pickups work best a few blocks out on the Grand Concourse or Jerome Avenue, and Citi Field and the tennis center clear faster from a corner out on Roosevelt Avenue.   • SoHo

    Evening pickups on the Broadway and Prince Street blocks after dinner.

How do I book a limousine in NYC?

Book online at customer.moovs.app or call +1 (646) 775-0556 with the pickup address, the destination, the date and time and the passenger count. Dispatch replies in writing with the vehicle class and the fare. Event work with several locations and a fixed ceremony time is easier to set up by phone.

How much does limousine service in NYC cost?

The Luxury Sprinter and every other class book by the hour. Every hourly booking carries a three hour minimum, and airport transfers are flat rated from any New York City address. See all rates.

Do you run stretch limousines?

No. The fleet runs high roof Sprinters and does not run stretch sedans. The Limo Sprinter and the Jet Sprinter each seat 12 with 10 large and 2 small bags, and the whole party boards through one sliding door with room to stand in the cabin.

How many passengers fit in a Sprinter?

Twelve. The Limo Sprinter and the Jet Sprinter each seat up to 12 passengers and take 10 large plus 2 small bags. They differ in the interior rather than the numbers, bench seating with a bar in one and two rows of captain's chairs in the other.

What are the rules on a prom or wedding booking?

Smoking is prohibited in every NYC LUX RIDE vehicle. Booking at least 24 hours ahead is recommended and secures both the vehicle class and the time slot. Infant, toddler and booster seats are available on request at no extra charge for family bookings.
